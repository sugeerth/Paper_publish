01. Cresta Senior MLE Interview Loop: Format, Rounds, Culture and the Senior Bar

How to read this chapter. This chapter puts the most weight on the ML system design round, as requested. It also covers the rounds around it: screens, coding, AI-assisted coding, deep dive and behavioral.

Tagging system. Every question carries one of two tags:

  • [Reported] [n]: a candidate report or question bank attributes this question, or this round type, to Cresta. The number points to the References list. Where the report is for another role (SWE, FDE), the tag says so.
  • [Likely]: an inference from Cresta's job postings, products and engineering blog. No candidate has reported it. A citation on a [Likely] item points to the product or posting the question is based on, not to a candidate report.

Research limits. None of the cited pages could be opened during research or during this review. The egress proxy refused connections to Glassdoor, Blind, 1point3acres, PracHub, Greenhouse, CoderPad and cresta.com, and the web-search budget for this review run was used up. Every [Reported] item therefore rests on search-result excerpts gathered while this bank was being researched (this chapter and chapters 02-07), not on reading the full pages. One reference that no excerpt supported has been replaced (see the end of the References). Confirm the loop with your recruiter.

Format notes

What candidates and sources report

  • Senior MLE loop (Glassdoor, 2025-26): the stages were a recruiter call, a LeetCode-medium technical round, a "vibe coding" round using an LLM tool, and a systems design round. The sample design prompt was "How would you deploy a model in production?" [1]
  • Live coding setting: reviewers describe live rounds of about an hour in Python on CoderPad over Zoom, with a software engineer, camera on, and conceptual AI questions mixed in [1].
  • Phone-screen pace: a 2026 Senior SWE report describes three LeetCode-medium questions in 70 minutes. The reviewer felt the interviewer wanted a specific approach, not just any working solution [31].
  • Senior/Staff SWE loop (Blind, 2024): one hour of coding, one hour of system design and a hiring-manager chat [3].
  • Mid-level SWE loop (Blind): one technical screen, three coding interviews and three behavioral rounds, with a mix of LeetCode-style and object-oriented questions. Another candidate's recruiter email said the interview would also cover "key concepts and fundamentals around LLMs, GenAI, RAG, and AI Agents" [3][4].
  • MLE phone screen, AI Agent direction (1point3acres, 2025): greedy and beam-search decoding over a next-token probability dictionary [28][10].
  • Async first step: Cresta adopted an asynchronous CoderPad technical assessment as the first engineering filter and runs its technical hiring on CoderPad [11]. A Cresta engineering posting describes a self-paced CoderPad assessment after resume review [26]. Blind posters mention an online assessment (OA) before the recruiter screen [5][6]. One SWE online challenge mixed data structures and algorithms with SQL joins [32].
  • AI-allowed practical round: a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) candidate described a 1.5-hour practical round with AI tools allowed [5]. This matches the "vibe coding" round in the senior MLE report [1].
  • System design with an agentic or eval flavor: PracHub lists a Cresta ML system design question, "Design an Evaluation Platform for AI Agents Across Prompt and Model Changes" [8][9].
  • Other Cresta engineering loops: reports include a project presentation round [2], a customer-delivery round plus a leadership round for a Senior FDE [2], an OOP pool/orchestrator coding question with round-robin scheduling [1], and a job dispatcher/executor phone screen (SWE, 2026) [29].
  • Timeline: about 25 days on average across roles, according to Glassdoor [2].
  • Policy: Cresta postings say candidates may not record interviews or use AI transcription during them, and that recruiting email comes only from @cresta.ai [12].

What the senior bar looks like (from postings and blog)

  • Strong ML foundations from before LLMs, combined with deep LLM and prompting expertise. The Agentic Assist track names both as requirements, along with "a proven ability to move research into production-grade systems" [16].
  • Evaluation of non-deterministic systems. Postings name offline benchmarking, online experimentation and LLM-as-a-judge, plus specific failure modes to diagnose: hallucinations, retrieval errors, tool misuse, context drift, prompt brittleness and multi-step reasoning breakdowns [14][12].
  • Quality metrics. Postings list accuracy, faithfulness, task completion, latency, cost and robustness [14].
  • Scale and production constraints. Postings ask candidates to design for scalability, latency, cost efficiency, security and maintainability. The Voice posting covers real-time inference and streaming pipelines [15].
  • Culture. Third-party pages cite these operating principles: "Mission is the boss", "Do too much", "Have the courage to be disliked" and "patient in strategy, impatient in execution". Reviewers describe a high technical bar, ownership, little politics and a fast pace [25][4].

Questions

A. ML system design: LLM agents and evaluation (highest priority)

  1. Design an evaluation platform for AI agents that detects regressions across prompt changes and model changes. Its users are the teams that build agents. [Reported] [8][9]
    • How do you version prompts, models, tools and test sets together so that a run is reproducible?
    • Which checks are deterministic, and which use an LLM judge? How do you calibrate the judge against human experts?
    • How do you score the agent's reasoning and tool-use path separately from whether the final outcome was correct?
    • How do you handle non-determinism? How many reruns per scenario, and what statistical test decides that something is a regression?
    • How do you gate a release in CI? What is the blocking threshold?
  2. How would you deploy a model in production? [Reported] [1]
    • What does your rollout look like: shadow, canary, A/B, then full?
    • What do you monitor in the first 24 hours, and what triggers a rollback?
    • How do you serve it within a real-time latency budget?
    • How do you handle model versioning and per-customer configuration?
  3. Design a customer simulator that generates realistic synthetic callers to test an AI agent before launch. [Likely] [17][27]
    • How do you give simulated callers objectives, personalities, account data and deviations from the standard path?
    • How do you mine real conversations so that the test set covers what production actually sees?
    • How do you stop the simulator and the agent from sharing the same blind spots?
  4. Design a layered ("Swiss-cheese") reliability strategy for a production AI agent: pre-launch tests, human review and production monitoring. [Likely] [18]
    • Which failure modes does each layer catch, and which does it miss?
    • How do you turn production failures back into regression tests?
  5. Design an LLM-as-a-judge system for scoring millions of conversations, with calibration to expert labels. [Likely]
    • How do you measure agreement between the judge and humans, and track it over time?
    • How do you detect judge drift when you upgrade the judge model?
    • What does the judge cost per conversation, and how do you cut it?
  6. Design an AI agent that fully handles customer voice or chat for an enterprise, including tool calls (order lookup, refunds) and escalation to a human. [Likely]
    • How do you constrain tool use so the agent cannot make unauthorized commitments?
    • How do you detect and recover from tool misuse or context drift in multi-step flows?
    • How do you design the handoff to a human agent, including the context you pass along?
  7. Design a multi-agent orchestration layer, in which a router hands off to specialized sub-agents. [Likely]
    • When does a multi-agent design beat a single agent with many tools?
    • How do you trace and debug a failure that spans several agents?
  8. Design a guardrail system against prompt injection and policy violations in a customer-facing agent. [Likely]
    • What does it add to latency, and where in the pipeline does it run?
  9. Design an "agent for building agents": a copilot that turns a business owner's natural-language description of a workflow (for example, flight rebooking) into an agent configuration, then tests it. [Likely] [34]
    • How do you validate the generated configuration before it touches real customers?
    • How do you keep a human in the loop without making the copilot pointless?
  10. Design a grounding checker that flags agent responses unsupported by the knowledge base or by tool outputs, before or right after they are sent. [Likely] [35]
    • Claim extraction plus entailment, or a single judge call? What does each cost in latency?
    • What do you do when the checker fires in the middle of a live voice turn?

B. ML system design: real-time and voice

  1. Design real-time Agent Assist: listen to a live call and surface next-best-action or knowledge suggestions to the human agent. [Likely] [21]
    • What is the end-to-end latency budget from audio to suggestion, and how is it split across ASR, retrieval and LLM?
    • How do you handle partial versus final transcripts? (Cresta pushes both about once per second [21].)
    • How do you decide when to show a suggestion and when to stay silent?
    • How do you measure the effect on agent outcomes, separating it from agent skill?
  2. Design a voice AI agent pipeline that responds in under a second: telephony, streaming ASR, LLM and TTS. [Likely] [19]
    • Which per-component metrics do you track (LLM first-token latency, TTS first-byte latency), and how do you measure end-to-end latency?
    • How do you detect end-of-turn and handle barge-in?
    • Which networking choices matter (connection reuse, streaming APIs, keeping DNS off the critical path)?
  3. Design an inference graph that runs several models (ASR, redaction, intent, sentiment, summarization) per conversation, with customer-specific policies. [Likely] [20]
    • How do you express dependencies and skip models a customer has not enabled?
    • How do you isolate a slow model so it cannot block real-time outputs?
  4. Design an ASR evaluation and quality-monitoring program for contact-center audio across accents, noise and domain vocabulary. [Likely] [13]
    • How does WER differ from downstream task quality, and which do you optimize?
    • How do you get ground-truth transcripts cheaply?
    • How do you detect ASR regressions for a single customer?
  5. Design per-customer ASR adaptation for product names, account IDs and jargon (contextual biasing, custom vocabularies or fine-tuning). [Likely] [13]
    • How do you stop biasing toward one customer's terms from raising errors on ordinary speech?
    • How do you roll out an adapted model to one tenant safely?
  6. Design a real-time sentiment or escalation-risk detector for live calls. [Likely]
    • How do you combine acoustic and lexical signals?
    • How do you set alert thresholds per customer?

C. ML system design: RAG, knowledge and analytics

  1. Design Knowledge Assist: RAG over a customer's knowledge base during live conversations. [Likely] [23][37]
    • How do you reformulate ambiguous conversational queries before retrieval?
    • Would you fine-tune embeddings for the domain, and how would you mine hard negatives?
    • How do you adapt answers by channel (voice, chat, email)?
    • How do you handle stale or contradictory documents?
  2. Design topic discovery over millions of unlabeled conversations: an LLM-built taxonomy with up to three levels, which admins can merge, split and rename. [Likely] [24]
    • How do you keep cluster assignments stable when the taxonomy is edited?
    • How do you overlay outcomes (resolution, sales) to rank problem areas?
  3. Design a system that infers conversation outcomes (resolved or not, customer experience) without surveys or disposition codes. [Likely] [24]
    • How do you validate inferred outcomes without ground truth?
  4. Design a research agent ("AI Analyst") that answers open-ended business questions over a customer's conversation corpus. [Likely] [24]
    • How do you combine per-conversation LLM extraction with aggregate statistics?
    • How do you stop the agent from presenting a weak correlation as a finding?
  5. Design automated QA scoring for 100% of calls against a customer-defined rubric. [Likely]
    • How do you turn a free-text rubric item into a reliable judgment?
    • How do you stop agents from gaming the scorer?
  6. Design a real-time trend detector that flags emerging issues before dashboards would show them. [Likely]
    • How do you separate a true spike from seasonality or noise?
  7. Design post-call summarization that writes into each customer's CRM format, using fine-tuned models rather than a frontier API. [Likely] [36]
    • How do you evaluate summaries when every customer wants different fields?
    • When does fine-tuning beat prompting a larger model for this task?

D. ML system design: models, serving, experiments and cost

  1. Design the domain adaptation of a base LLM for contact centers, in the spirit of Cresta's Ocean-1: data, SFT, preference tuning and serving. [Likely] [22]
    • Self-instruct and RLHF/DPO: what data do you need, and how do you avoid overfitting to one customer?
    • How do you prove it beats a frontier API on quality, latency and cost?
  2. Design a migration from a frontier LLM API to a smaller self-hosted model for one high-volume feature. [Likely]
    • Which quality bar must be met, and how is it measured?
    • Distillation or fine-tuning: which, and on what data?
  3. Design multi-tenant serving: one base model with per-customer adapters or prompts. [Likely] [38]
    • How do you serve hundreds of LoRA adapters efficiently?
    • How do you prevent data leakage across tenants?
  4. Design an LLM serving stack for real-time features with p99 latency targets. [Likely]
    • What do batching, KV cache, quantization and speculative decoding each trade off?
    • How do you autoscale when CPU-based autoscaling (HPA) is meaningless for GPUs?
  5. Design monitoring for production LLM features across hundreds of enterprise customers. [Likely]
    • Which label-free signals catch quality regressions?
    • How do you catch a regression that affects only one customer?
  6. Design the feedback flywheel: agent accept, edit and ignore signals on suggestions feed model improvement. [Likely]
    • What biases does this implicit feedback introduce, and how do you correct them?
  7. Design a PII redaction pipeline for audio and transcripts that runs before storage and training. [Likely]
    • How do you trade recall against precision, and what does a miss cost?
  8. Design an online experiment for a new Agent Assist model when a handful of large customers dominate traffic and agents work in teams. [Likely]
    • Randomize by conversation, by agent or by team? What interference do you expect?
    • Which outcome metric moves fast enough to decide within two weeks?
  9. Design the training-data pipeline for customer-specific models under contractual data limits: opt-outs, retention windows and deletion requests. [Likely]
    • How do you prove a deleted customer's data no longer influences a deployed model?

E. Coding rounds (online assessment, technical screen and onsite)

  1. Implement greedy decoding given a Python dictionary that maps each token to its candidate next tokens and their probabilities, starting from a start token and stopping at an end token or a maximum length. Then extend it to beam search with beam width k. [Reported] [10][28]
    • How do you add length normalization?
    • How would you add top-k / top-p sampling and temperature?
    • What is the time and space complexity in beam width and vocabulary size?
  2. Design the decoder as a reusable component: what interface lets greedy and beam search share code? [Reported] [30]
  3. Walk through how an AI agent generates a sequence step by step. Where can the decoding strategy change the agent's behavior? [Reported] [28]
  4. Solve LeetCode-medium problems in the technical round, possibly three in 70 minutes. [Reported] [1][31]
    • Can you state the complexity and test edge cases before running the code?
    • If the interviewer is steering toward a specific approach, how do you find out early?
  5. Implement a worker pool and an orchestrator that assigns tasks round-robin (reported for the FDE AI Agent role); a SWE variant asks for a job dispatcher that hands jobs to a pool of executors. [Reported] [1][29]
    • How do you handle worker failures and retries?
    • How do you make it thread-safe?
  6. Implement a rate limiter in your language of choice. [Reported] [1]
    • Extend it to per-tenant limits for shared LLM API calls. Token bucket or sliding window, and why?
  7. Call a public API (Wikipedia was used in a reported SWE phone screen), then process and return the results. [Reported] [7]
    • How do you handle pagination, rate limits and errors?
  8. Implement autocomplete: given a list of words and a prefix, return the matching suggestions (reported for SWE). [Reported] [32]
    • How do you rank suggestions by frequency, and what changes at a million words?
  9. Convert a flat list of records with parent references into a tree (reported for SWE). [Reported] [32]
  10. Solve a grid or matrix problem framed as a puzzle game in a timed online challenge (reported for SWE). [Reported] [32]
  11. Write SQL with joins in the online challenge, for example: from conversations and annotations tables, find agents whose suggestion-acceptance rate dropped week over week. [Reported for SQL joins; the example prompt is Likely] [32]
  12. Solve object-oriented design problems mixed with LeetCode-style problems (mid-level SWE loop). [Reported] [3]
  13. Implement word error rate (edit distance) between a reference transcript and a hypothesis. [Likely]
  14. Given a stream of utterances with speaker labels and timestamps, merge them into turns and emit partial results in real time. [Likely]
  15. Implement precision, recall and F1 from scratch for multi-label intent classification, plus micro and macro averages. [Likely]
  16. Implement cosine-similarity top-k retrieval over an embedding matrix in NumPy, without loops. [Likely]
    • Which ANN index would you choose at 100M vectors?
  17. Implement a dynamic batching queue that maximizes GPU throughput under a max-latency constraint. [Likely]
  18. Implement a streaming chunker that splits a live transcript into token-budgeted segments without cutting sentences. [Likely]
  19. Count the top-k most frequent intents over a sliding time window on an unbounded stream, with bounded memory. [Likely]

F. AI-assisted ("vibe coding") practical round

  1. Build a small working feature end to end with an LLM coding tool, within the time limit. [Reported] [1][5]
    • How do you break the task down before prompting the tool?
    • How do you verify generated code? Which tests do you write yourself?
    • When do you stop prompting and write the code by hand?
  2. Build a minimal RAG or agent prototype (for example, an agent that answers from a document set or calls a mock API). [Likely]
    • How do you evaluate it in the time available?
  3. Build a tool-calling loop for a flight-rebooking agent against mock flight and booking APIs, including a confirmation step before any change. [Likely] [34]
  4. Build a small evaluation harness: run an LLM over a labeled set of transcripts, enforce a JSON output schema, and report accuracy with confidence intervals. [Likely]
  5. Debug an LLM-generated pipeline that passes its tests but gives wrong results. [Likely]

G. LLM / GenAI fundamentals (flagged by recruiters)

  1. Explain the key concepts and fundamentals of LLMs, GenAI, RAG and AI agents. [Reported] [3][4]
  2. What is RAG, and how do you prevent hallucination? [Reported for an FDE technical screen] [33]
    • In what ways does RAG still fail?
    • How do you measure faithfulness or groundedness automatically?
  3. What is an AI agent, as opposed to a chain or a workflow? When would you not use an agent? [Likely]
  4. How does function calling / tool use work, and how do you test multi-turn agent trajectories? [Likely]
  5. What drives LLM inference latency and cost, and which levers reduce them? [Likely]
  6. How do you handle conversations longer than the context window? [Likely]
  7. Compare SFT, DPO and RLHF. When is each worth its cost? [Likely]
  8. How do you make prompts robust across model versions, and how do you regression-test them? [Likely]
  9. Which ML fundamentals from before LLMs still matter here (calibration, class imbalance, active learning, experiment design)? [Likely] [16]
    • Why does calibration matter for a confidence-gated suggestion?
  10. How do ASR errors propagate into downstream NLP, and how do you make classifiers robust to them? [Likely]
  11. How do you evaluate a generative model when there is no single correct answer? [Likely]
    • How do you design a rubric for human raters and measure agreement between them?
  12. QA labels from different customers' reviewers disagree systematically. How do you detect and model annotator bias before training on the labels? [Likely]

02. Coding: Reported Cresta Problems and Practical ML-Adjacent Python

Format notes

  • Platform and setting. Cresta runs its technical hiring on CoderPad, including an asynchronous CoderPad Screen step for engineering roles [11]. Candidates describe live rounds as about an hour on CoderPad and Zoom with a software engineer, with the camera on. The coding is live Python, and conceptual AI questions are mixed in [6].
  • MLE phone screen. An August 2025 1point3acres report on the MLE (AI Agent) phone screen describes one problem in two parts: sequence decoding over a dictionary of next-token probabilities, first with greedy search and then with beam search, written in Python [1][2]. PracHub lists the same problem under two Cresta-tagged titles [4].
  • Loop shape. A Glassdoor report for a Senior ML Engineer lists a recruiter call, a LeetCode-medium technical interview, a "vibe coding" interview (building something with an LLM coding tool) and a system design round [6]. SWE loops are described as one technical screen, three coding rounds and three behavioral rounds, mixing LeetCode-style and object-oriented problems [8]. A Senior/Staff loop is described as one hour of coding, one hour of system design and a hiring-manager chat [9].
  • Difficulty and bar. A 2026 Toronto Senior SWE report describes a phone screen with three LeetCode-medium-level questions in 70 minutes [5]. One reviewer felt the interviewer wanted a specific approach, not just any working solution [5]. Other reported practical prompts include a rate limiter [6], a job dispatcher/executor system (SWE phone screen, 2026) [3], autocomplete over a word list, and turning a flat list into a tree [7]. FDE candidates report a 1.5-hour practical round where AI tools are allowed [10].
  • Senior-level emphasis (inferred). Expect clean, testable Python, explicit complexity trade-offs, and extensions toward streaming and production concerns such as partial transcripts, latency and concurrency. These map to Cresta's real-time voice and agent stack [13][14][15] and its Senior MLE postings (Python/Go, PyTorch, Transformers, LLMs, model optimization) [16][17].

Tag legend: [Reported] means a source reports the question or a very close variant for Cresta (cited). [Likely] means it is inferred from the role, the team and Cresta's products; it is not reported.

A. Sequence decoding (the reported MLE screen problem)

A1. Core problem

  1. [Reported] You are given a Python dict that maps each token to a dict of candidate next tokens and their probabilities. Starting from a start token, implement greedy decoding that produces the most likely sequence until an end token or a maximum length [1][4].
    • What do you do when a token has no outgoing edges, or when the graph has a cycle?
    • How do you break ties between equal probabilities so the output is deterministic?
    • What is the time complexity per step, and overall?
  2. [Reported] Extend your solution to beam search with beam width k over the same next-token dictionary, and return the top sequence or the top-k sequences with their scores [1][4].
    • Why do you accumulate log-probabilities instead of multiplying probabilities?
    • How do you handle beams that reach the end token before the others do?
    • What is the complexity in terms of k, the vocabulary branching factor and the maximum length?
  3. [Reported] Design the decoder as a reusable component (PracHub title: "Design sequence decoding with greedy and beam search"). What interface would you expose so that greedy and beam share code [4]?
    • How would you plug in a real model's next-token function instead of a static dict?
    • How would you unit-test the decoder?

A2. Likely extensions of the decoding problem

  1. [Likely] Add length normalization to beam search (for example, dividing by length^alpha). Show a case where the unnormalized beam prefers a short sequence.
    • Does normalization change which hypotheses get pruned mid-search, or only the final ranking?
  2. [Likely] Implement top-k sampling and nucleus (top-p) sampling with temperature over the same structure.
    • How do you make the sampler reproducible with seeds in tests?
    • What happens when p is so small that only one token survives?
  3. [Likely] Add a constraint so that a banned set of tokens (for example, PII placeholders or forbidden phrases) never appears in decoded output.
    • How would you enforce "no repeated n-gram of size n"?
  4. [Likely] Return the k best complete sequences without duplicates when different paths produce the same surface string.
  5. [Likely] Implement early stopping: terminate beam search once no live beam can beat the best finished hypothesis.
    • Under what scoring assumption is this stopping rule correct?
  6. [Likely] Batch the decoder so it decodes many prompts in parallel with a vectorized next-token function, using NumPy.
    • How do you handle sequences in the batch that finish at different steps (masking or padding)?
  7. [Likely] Implement constrained decoding to one of a fixed set of allowed outputs (for example, intent labels) using a trie of allowed token sequences.
  8. [Likely] Make the decoder stream tokens to a caller as a generator while beam search is still running.
    • Can you safely emit tokens before the beam has converged? Which prefix is stable?

B. Streaming text and transcripts

  1. [Likely] You receive ASR results as a stream of events (utterance_id, is_final, text, start_ms, end_ms). Partial transcripts can be revised until a final arrives. Maintain the current best transcript of the conversation. (Grounded in Cresta's partial-vs-final transcript pipeline [13][14].)
    • How do you handle a final that arrives after a newer partial for the same utterance?
    • What is your memory footprint for a 2-hour call?
  2. [Likely] Given the transcript stream, fire a callback only when a partial transcript is "stable", for example when a prefix has not changed across N consecutive partials.
    • How do you avoid re-firing for the same stable prefix?
  3. [Likely] Merge two ordered streams of utterances (agent channel and customer channel) into a single time-ordered conversation, interleaving by start time.
    • How would you generalize this to k speakers? What is the complexity with a heap?
    • How do you handle overlapping speech?
  4. [Likely] Implement a sliding-window keyword detector over a stream of tokens: emit an event when any of M keyword phrases appears. (Cresta runs cheap keyword detection on partials [14].)
    • How does Aho-Corasick compare to naive matching as M grows?
    • What if keywords can span two transcript chunks?
  5. [Likely] Implement a rolling context window that keeps the last N tokens (or last T seconds) of a conversation to feed an LLM prompt.
    • How do you always keep the system prompt and the first customer statement?
  6. [Likely] Write a function that deduplicates overlapping chunks of transcript text from a streaming source (chunk i+1 may repeat the tail of chunk i).
    • What is the complexity of your overlap detection? Can you use KMP or rolling hashes?
  7. [Likely] Compute talk-time, silence and interruption statistics per speaker from a list of timestamped segments.
    • How do you define and count an interruption?
    • How do you handle segments with missing end times?
  8. [Likely] Implement real-time redaction of credit-card numbers and SSNs in a token stream where a number can be split across chunks. (Cresta's voice stack includes audio and transcript redaction [13].)
    • How much lookahead buffer do you need, and what does it cost in latency?
    • How would you validate card numbers (Luhn) without false positives on phone numbers?
  9. [Likely] Given word-level ASR timestamps and a list of detected "moments" (character spans), map each moment back to audio start and end times.
  10. [Likely] Implement a debouncer/throttler so that a downstream model is called at most once every X ms per conversation while still processing the latest partial.
  11. [Likely] Implement an online running mean and variance (Welford) of per-turn sentiment scores per conversation, and flag turns that deviate by more than k standard deviations.

C. Practical systems coding (reported practical prompts and close variants)

  1. [Reported] Implement a rate limiter in your language of choice [6].
    • Token bucket versus sliding-window log versus fixed window: which did you pick and why?
    • How would you make it thread-safe?
    • How do you make it per-customer (tenant) and per-endpoint?
  2. [Reported] Implement a job dispatcher/executor system: a dispatcher accepts jobs and hands them to a pool of executors (reported for a 2026 SWE phone screen) [3].
    • How do you handle job failures and retries with backoff?
    • How do you support priorities, or cancellation of a queued job?
    • How do you guarantee at-least-once versus exactly-once execution?
  3. [Reported] Implement an API client exercise: call a public API (reported as a Wikipedia API task in an SWE phone screen) and process the results [2].
    • How do you handle pagination, rate limits and transient errors?
    • How would you parallelize requests and cap concurrency?
  4. [Likely] Implement an inference request batcher (dynamic batching): collect requests until either batch size B or wait time T, then call the model once and route results back to callers.
    • How do you choose B and T against a latency SLO? (Cresta's voice-agent target is about 1.5 s end to end [15].)
    • What happens when the model call raises for one item in the batch?
  5. [Likely] Implement an LRU cache with TTL for embedding or LLM responses keyed by normalized prompt.
    • How do you make get and put O(1)?
    • How would you add a maximum size in bytes instead of in entries?
  6. [Likely] Write a retry decorator with exponential backoff, jitter and a timeout, usable on both sync and async functions.
  7. [Likely] Implement a simple inference-graph executor: given a DAG of model "nodes" with dependencies, run them in topological order and run independent nodes concurrently. (Cresta describes inference graphs that orchestrate ML services [14].)
    • How do you detect cycles?
    • How do you skip nodes whose conditions are not met, for example models that run only on final transcripts?
  8. [Likely] Implement a circuit breaker around an external LLM or ASR provider call.
    • What state transitions exist, and how do you test them deterministically?
  9. [Likely] Write an asyncio producer/consumer pipeline: ingest transcript events, run a classifier and write annotations, with bounded queues for backpressure.
    • What happens when the consumer is slower than the producer?
  10. [Likely] Implement a top-K heavy-hitters tracker (for example, most frequent customer intents in the last hour) over a stream.
    • Exact (heap plus counter with time buckets) versus approximate (Count-Min Sketch): when would you choose each?
  11. [Likely] Implement consistent hashing to route conversations to workers so that a conversation's events always land on the same worker.
    • What happens when a worker is added or removed?

  5. [Likely] Given a conversation as a list of turns, find the longest contiguous segment where the customer's sentiment never drops below a threshold.
  6. [Likely] Merge overlapping time intervals (speaker segments), then compute the total non-overlapping speech duration.
  7. [Likely] Find all pairs of conversations whose embedding cosine similarity exceeds a threshold, efficiently for N = 100k.

  8. [Likely] Implement word-level edit distance and use it to compute word error rate (WER) between a reference and an ASR hypothesis.

  9. [Likely] Longest common subsequence between two transcripts of the same call from two different ASR vendors.
  10. [Likely] Group near-duplicate customer utterances using MinHash/LSH or normalized string keys.
  11. [Likely] Given a log of (conversation_id, timestamp, event) events, find conversations where event B happened within T seconds after event A.
  12. [Likely] Design a data structure supporting add(word) and most_frequent_in_last(seconds) over a stream.
  13. [Likely] Validate balanced spans: given nested annotation spans over text (entities inside sentences), check that they are properly nested.
  14. [Likely] Tokenize text with a given byte-pair-encoding (BPE) merge table: apply the merges in order to a word.

03. ML/LLM Depth for Contact-Center AI: NLP, Speech, Fine-Tuning, Evaluation, RAG, Agents and Latency

Format notes for this round

  • Where depth gets tested. No source reports a standalone "ML deep dive" round for this role. Expect the depth questions below inside the phone screen, the system design round and the hiring-manager conversation.
  • Duration and interviewer. The reported MLE phone screen (AI Agent direction) was a live Python decoding problem, with follow-ups on how an agent generates sequences [6][4][5]. Interviewers are probably ML engineers from the team you would join. This is an inference from the postings for the Voice Experience, ASR, Agentic Assist and Staff tracks [22][23][24][25][26].
  • Topics recruiters flag in advance. A recruiter email to a candidate said the interviews would cover "key concepts and fundamentals around LLMs, GenAI, RAG, and AI Agents" [3].
  • Senior-level bar, as inferred from Cresta's postings.
    • Debug hallucinations, retrieval errors, tool misuse, context drift, prompt brittleness and multi-step reasoning failures [22][23].
    • Evaluate with offline benchmarks, online experiments and LLM-as-a-judge [22][26].
    • Design and deploy enterprise RAG [22].
    • Make trade-offs across the real-time voice stack: ASR, turn detection, LLM and TTS [13][24].
  • Products and tech to anchor your answers.
    • Ocean-1, Cresta's contact-center foundation model [11].
    • Fine-tuned models that power Knowledge Assist, summarization and chat suggestions [8][9].
    • Thousands of per-customer LoRA adapters served on one base-model cluster at Fireworks [9][10].
    • The voice stack and inference graphs [14][15].
    • LLM-judge evaluators, automated agent testing and the "Swiss-cheese" evaluation model [16][17][19].
    • Hallucination grounding [18], Cresta Conductor [20] and Insights [21].

Decoding and LLM fundamentals

  1. Implement greedy decoding and beam search over a next-token probability table given as a Python dictionary (a directed token graph). [Reported] [4][5][6]
    • How do you handle sequences that hit an end token at different lengths? Would you apply length normalization?
    • What is the time and memory complexity in terms of beam width, vocabulary size and depth?
    • When does beam search do worse than greedy decoding for open-ended dialogue?
  2. Walk through how an AI agent generates a sequence step by step. Where can decoding strategy change the agent's behavior? [Reported] [6]
    • How would you add constraints, such as forcing a valid tool-call JSON schema?
  3. Explain the key concepts and fundamentals of LLMs, GenAI, RAG and AI agents. Then go one level deeper: what in the transformer architecture makes in-context learning and tool use possible? [Reported] [3]
    • Where do pretraining, instruction tuning and preference tuning each change model behavior?
  4. Compare temperature, top-k, top-p and min-p sampling. Which would you use for agent-assist suggestions, and which for summaries? [Likely]
    • How do you make outputs reproducible for regression tests?
  5. Explain speculative decoding. When would a draft model trained on contact-center data pay off? [Likely] [10]
    • What acceptance rate makes it worthwhile, and how does batch size change that?
  6. Explain the KV cache and how its memory grows. How do paged attention and prefix caching apply to long call transcripts? [Likely]
    • Estimate the KV-cache memory for a 7B-class model with 8k context and 200 concurrent sessions. Which knobs (GQA, FP8 cache, context truncation) would you turn first?
  7. How do rotary position embeddings work, and how would you extend a model's context window for hour-long calls? What quality risks come with context extension? [Likely]
  8. Tokenizers often split account numbers, SKUs, phone numbers and dates badly. How does this hurt extraction and generation, and how would you mitigate it? [Likely] [12]

NLP for conversations

  1. What is RAG, and how do you prevent hallucination? [Reported] [2] (reported for a Forward Deployed Engineer technical screen)
    • In what ways does RAG still fail?
    • How would you detect a hallucination that got past your safeguards?
  2. How would you build intent or topic discovery over millions of unlabeled call transcripts? [Likely] [21]
    • How do you pick the number of clusters and keep them stable across retrains?
    • LLM labeling of every conversation is expensive at Insights scale. Where would you use embeddings and clustering, and where an LLM?
  3. Design a classifier for conversation "moments", such as a missed upsell or a compliance phrase not said. Would you choose a fine-tuned encoder or a prompted LLM? [Likely] [8]
    • How do you handle severe class imbalance and labels that change by customer?
  4. You need training labels for a new per-customer classifier and have only a few hundred human labels. How would you use LLM-generated (weak) labels, and how do you measure and correct their noise? [Likely] [8]
  5. A model's confidence threshold works for one customer and over-fires for another. How do you calibrate scores per customer, and how do you set thresholds when you have few positives? [Likely]
  6. How would you predict escalation, churn risk or negative sentiment early in a live conversation, from partial transcripts? [Likely] [14]
    • How do you trade earliness against precision, and how do you evaluate a prediction made at minute 3 of a 20-minute call?
  7. How do transcription errors from ASR propagate into NLP models trained on clean text? How would you make the downstream models robust to them? [Likely] [15]
  8. How would you do PII detection and redaction on streaming transcripts with low latency? [Likely]
    • What precision/recall trade-off would you choose, and why?
    • How do you align redaction spans back to audio timestamps, so the recording is redacted too?
  9. How do you model speaker roles and turn structure in a two-party transcript for summarization or extraction? [Likely]
  10. How would you handle multilingual calls, and calls that switch language mid-conversation? [Likely]
  11. You fine-tuned a model on one customer's data and it looks great offline. How do you detect overfitting, leakage across time or agents, and memorization of that customer's PII? [Likely]

Speech and ASR

  1. Explain CTC, RNN-T and attention encoder-decoder ASR. Which suits streaming contact-center audio, and why? [Likely] [25]
  2. Streaming ASR returns partial transcripts every ~0.5 to 1.5 seconds and revises them. How should downstream models consume these unstable partials? [Likely] [15]
    • When do you trigger an LLM on partial text versus final text?
    • How do you upsert or reconcile results that were computed on revised partials?
  3. How do you measure ASR quality beyond WER for downstream tasks such as entity capture (account numbers, names, SKUs)? [Likely] [25]
    • How would you build a domain-specific test set, and how often would you refresh it?
  4. Spoken digits, dates, currency and spelled-out emails must become structured text. How would you design and evaluate inverse text normalization and entity formatting for ASR output? [Likely] [25]
  5. How would you adapt an ASR model to customer-specific vocabulary: contextual biasing, fine-tuning, or LM rescoring? [Likely] [25]
  6. You must choose between third-party ASR vendors and an in-house model (for example, a fine-tuned Whisper-class model). How would you run the bake-off? [Likely] [15][25]
    • How do you catch failure modes WER hides, such as hallucinated text during silence or hold music?
  7. Design end-of-turn (endpointing) detection for a voice AI agent. Compare silence-based VAD, acoustic models and semantic models. [Likely] [13][24]
    • How do you measure false barge-ins versus added latency?
    • How would you distill a large turn-detection model into one small enough for real time?
  8. Handling 8kHz telephony audio, noise and cross-talk: what augmentation and front-end choices matter? [Likely]
  9. Speaker diarization on stereo versus mono call recordings: how do the approaches differ, and how do you evaluate them? [Likely]
  10. How would you evaluate and choose a TTS system for a voice agent? Cover naturalness, pronunciation of names and alphanumerics, latency to first audio, and streaming behavior. [Likely] [13][24]
  11. What changes when you use speech-to-speech models instead of a cascaded ASR, LLM and TTS stack? [Likely] [24]
    • How do you evaluate quality and controllability in each design?
  12. The ASR WebSocket drops in the middle of a call. How would you replay or recover audio so the models don't lose context? [Likely] [15]

LLM fine-tuning and adaptation

  1. Cresta fine-tuned its own models to match GPT-4 quality on Knowledge Assist at lower cost and latency. How would you set up that project end to end? [Likely] [8][9]
    • How do you decide a fine-tuned model is "as good as" the frontier model?
  2. Ocean-1 is described as a contact-center foundation model. When would you do continued pretraining on domain conversations rather than only instruction tuning? How would you choose the data mix? [Likely] [11]
  3. Compare full fine-tuning, LoRA, QLoRA and prompt tuning for per-customer adaptation. [Likely] [9][10]
    • How do you choose LoRA rank and target modules?
  4. You serve thousands of per-customer LoRA adapters on one base model. What are the training, versioning and serving implications? [Likely] [10]
    • How do you handle a customer with too little data for its own adapter?
    • What breaks if you upgrade the base model?
    • How would you automatically evaluate and gate thousands of adapters before each release? [17]
  5. How do you build SFT data from contact-center transcripts? Cover label sources, agent-quality filtering and deduplication. [Likely] [8]
  6. When would you train on synthetic conversations generated by a stronger LLM? How do you keep synthetic data diverse, and how do you detect that it is degrading the model? [Likely] [17][20]
  7. When would you use preference optimization (DPO, RLHF, or RLAIF) for agent-assist suggestions? Where would the preference signal come from? [Likely]
    • Can you use agent accept/reject clicks as rewards? What biases do they carry?
  8. How do you prevent catastrophic forgetting of instruction following when you fine-tune on narrow domain data? [Likely] [11]
  9. Walk through the GPU memory math for fine-tuning a 7B and a 70B model. When do you need FSDP or ZeRO, activation checkpointing or mixed precision? [Likely] [23]
  10. How would you fine-tune an embedding model for contact-center terminology, product SKUs and policy language? [Likely] [12]
    • How do you mine hard negatives?
  11. When would you call a frontier API rather than run a self-hosted fine-tuned model? Discuss cost, latency, data residency and quality. [Likely] [27]

Distillation and model compression

  1. Explain knowledge distillation for LLMs: logit-level versus sequence-level (training on teacher outputs). [Likely]

  2. Design a pipeline that distills a large teacher into a small model for real-time summaries or suggestions. [Likely] [8]
    • How do you choose teacher prompts and filter teacher outputs?
    • How do you know when the student is good enough to ship?
  3. An LLM judge is too slow and expensive to score every production conversation. How would you distill it into a small classifier for online monitoring, and how do you keep the two aligned over time? [Likely] [16]
  4. Compare quantization methods (INT8, FP8, 4-bit GPTQ/AWQ) in terms of quality loss and throughput. How does quantization interact with LoRA adapters? [Likely] [10]
  5. When would you prune or use a smaller architecture rather than distill? [Likely]
  6. How do you detect regressions in rare but high-stakes behaviors (such as compliance disclosures) after compression? [Likely]

Retrieval-augmented generation

  1. Design a RAG system for Knowledge Assist that answers agent questions mid-call from a customer's knowledge base. [Likely] [8][12][22]
    • How do you reformulate the query from a noisy, partial conversation?
    • How do you choose chunk size and structure for knowledge-base articles, FAQs and policy PDFs?
  2. Knowledge Assist can surface answers before the agent asks. How do you decide when to trigger retrieval proactively, and how do you keep false triggers from distracting agents? [Likely] [9]
  3. Compare hybrid retrieval (BM25 plus dense) with dense only for domain jargon and SKUs. [Likely] [12]
  4. Where would you add a reranker (cross-encoder or LLM-based), and how do you fit it into a real-time latency budget? [Likely] [12][13]
  5. How do you evaluate retrieval separately from generation? Which metrics, and where do labels come from? [Likely]
    • How do you detect knowledge-base gaps, where the answer simply doesn't exist?
  6. Knowledge bases are often stale or contradictory. How do you make them "AI-ready", and how do you handle conflicting sources at answer time? [Likely] [12]
  7. How do you keep the index fresh when articles change daily? What does upgrading the embedding model cost, and how do you re-index without downtime? [Likely] [12]
  8. How do you enforce grounding and citation? When should the model refuse or fall back? [Likely] [18]
  9. How do you adapt answer length and format by channel (voice, chat, email)? [Likely] [12]
  10. Diagnose this: RAG answers look fluent but agents report they are wrong 15% of the time. Walk through your debugging. [Likely] [22]
    • How do you tell retrieval failures from generation failures?
  11. When would you use long-context stuffing instead of retrieval? [Likely]
  12. How do you handle multi-tenant isolation in a vector index, so no customer can retrieve another's documents? [Likely]

Agents and tool use

  1. Explain function calling end to end. How do you make tool-call arguments reliable? [Likely] [22]
  2. Tool calls fail, time out or are retried. How do you design tool schemas and error handling so the agent recovers, and so non-idempotent actions (refunds, cancellations) never run twice? [Likely] [23]
  3. Design a customer-facing AI agent that authenticates a caller, looks up an order and processes a refund. [Likely]
    • Where do deterministic guardrails sit, and where does the LLM decide?
    • How do you prevent a refund from running without verified identity?
  4. A customer's standard operating procedure has many branches. Would you encode it as an explicit workflow graph, a single long prompt, or a router over sub-agents? Which fails least in a regulated setting? [Likely] [26]
  5. What are the most common agent failure modes in production? How would you detect each one (tool misuse, context drift, looping, multi-step reasoning breakdown)? [Likely] [22][23]
  6. Explain AI agent frameworks and planning patterns (ReAct, plan-and-execute, state machines). Which fits a regulated contact center, and why? [Likely] [22]
  7. How do you manage memory and context across a 30-minute call, including summarization of earlier turns? [Likely]

  10. Cresta Conductor is described as an agent that helps build AI agents. How would you evaluate an LLM that writes or edits another agent's prompts and configuration? [Likely] [20]

  11. Agentic assist (agent-facing) versus AI agent (customer-facing): how do the risk tolerance, latency budget and evaluation differ? [Likely] [22][24][26]
