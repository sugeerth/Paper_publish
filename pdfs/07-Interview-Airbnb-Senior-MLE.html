01. Loop Format and the Senior Bar: Airbnb Senior MLE (Experimentation / A/B Testing)

Research note (read first). Live web research was not possible when this chapter was written. The shared web-search budget was used up, and the egress proxy refused connections to igotanoffer.com, interviewing.io, careers.airbnb.com and airbnb.tech. So this chapter draws on what was already known about Airbnb's widely reported interview process and on the sources listed under References. None of those sources were re-fetched for this chapter. Before relying on a URL or a "[Reported]" tag, open the source and confirm it. A recruiter's prep packet always overrides this chapter. Tags: [Reported] means a public source or the LeetCode Airbnb company tag attributes the item to Airbnb. [Likely] means an inference from the role, the level and Airbnb's products. It does not mean anyone reported it.

Format notes

Stage Typical length Interviewer What is assessed (Senior / L5 bar)
Recruiter call 20-30 min Recruiter Motivation ("Why Airbnb?"), level calibration (L5 = Senior), team fit (Search/Ranking, Pricing, Trust, Payments, Relevance, Experimentation platform), comp, timeline [1][2]
Technical phone screen 45-60 min Engineer One or two practical coding problems on a shared editor (CoderPad-style). The code is expected to compile and run against test cases. Pseudocode does not pass [1][2][3]
Onsite: Coding x2 45-60 min each Engineers Runnable, clean, tested code. One round is often algorithmic (LeetCode-medium/hard, many Airbnb-tagged problems). The other is a longer "practical" problem with evolving requirements (parsing, pagination, scheduling, simulation) [1][3][4]
Onsite: ML design 45-60 min Senior/Staff MLE End-to-end ML system for an Airbnb product (search ranking, pricing, fraud, recommendations), including offline/online evaluation and experiment design [Likely; 6-10]
Onsite: System design 45-60 min Senior/Staff engineer Scalable services and data pipelines (for example a booking system, an experimentation/assignment service, a feature store) [1][2]
Onsite: Past project deep dive 45-60 min Hiring manager or senior IC Ownership, technical depth, trade-offs and measured impact of a project you led. Sometimes presented with slides [1][2]
Onsite: Core values x2 30-45 min each Employees outside the hiring team (often non-engineers) Airbnb's four core values: Champion the Mission, Be a Host, Embrace the Adventure, Be a "Cereal" Entrepreneur. A strong fail here can veto an otherwise strong loop [1][2][5]
Debrief / hiring committee n/a Panel + leadership review Calibrated on level. Senior candidates are expected to drive ambiguity, lead cross-functional work and show measured impact [1][2]

Key expectations that candidates consistently report:

  • Runnable code is mandatory. Interviewers run your code. Syntax errors, unhandled edge cases or no tests count against you even if the logic is right [1][3].
  • Practical over puzzle. Problems are often framed as real product scenarios: listings pagination, host search results, price rounding, travel itineraries, calendar availability [3][4].
  • Core values are scored as seriously as the technical rounds and are run by people with no stake in the team [1][5].
  • Senior bar (L5): you independently own an ambiguous problem end to end. You make clear trade-offs (latency vs. quality, bias vs. variance in experiment estimates). You influence partners such as Product, Data Science and Design, and you mentor others. Coding and design are expected to be done without heavy hints [Likely; 1][2].
  • Experimentation-flavored MLE roles: Airbnb runs a mature in-house experimentation platform (ERF) and has written publicly about A/B testing pitfalls, interleaving for search ranking and two-sided-marketplace effects. Expect experimentation probes inside the ML design and past-project rounds [6][7][8][9].

Questions

A. Recruiter call and process logistics

  1. Why Airbnb, and why now? [Reported] [1][2]
    • Which Airbnb product surface (Search, Pricing, Trust, Experiences, Payments) would you most want to work on, and why?
    • Have you used Airbnb as a guest or as a host? What would you change?
  2. Walk me through your background in under three minutes, focusing on ML in production. [Reported] [1]
  3. What level are you targeting, and what scope of work did you own at your current level? [Likely]
    • How many engineers or scientists did you lead or influence on your largest project?
  4. Which programming language will you use in coding rounds, and are you fluent enough to run code without IDE help? [Likely] [3]
  5. How much of your recent work was modeling, and how much was infrastructure or experimentation? [Likely]
  6. Have you designed or analyzed online A/B tests yourself, or did a data scientist own that? [Likely]
  7. What are your compensation expectations and timeline, and do you have competing processes? [Reported] [2]
  8. Are you open to team matching across Search/Relevance, Pricing, Trust and the Experimentation platform? [Likely]

B. Technical phone screen (practical coding, must run)

  1. Given a list of listings (host_id, listing_id, score, city) and a page size, return paginated results so that no host appears twice on the same page if possible, preserving score order. [Reported] [3][4]
    • What happens on the last page when only one host's listings remain?
    • What is the time complexity, and can you do it in a single pass?
  2. Minimize rounding error: round a list of prices to integers so the sum equals a target, minimizing the total absolute rounding error. [Reported] [4]
    • What if the target is unreachable? How do you signal that?
    • How would you handle currency with two decimal places?
  3. Pour water: simulate water droplets falling onto an elevation map at index K, with the leftward-first flow rule. [Reported] [4]
    • How would you print the final terrain and water visually?
    • How do you test the boundary indices?
  4. Text justification: fully justify lines of words to a fixed width. [Reported] [4]
    • How do you handle a single word on a line, and the last line?
  5. Flatten a 2D vector into an iterator with next, hasNext and (as a follow-up) remove. [Reported] [4]
  6. Implement a CSV parser that handles quoted fields, escaped quotes and commas inside quotes. [Reported] [3]
    • How do you handle multi-line quoted fields?
  7. Given an IP address and a count, return the minimal list of CIDR blocks covering exactly that range. [Reported] [4]
  8. Write tests for your solution right now. Which cases did you choose, and why? [Likely] [3]

C. Onsite coding round 1 (algorithmic, Airbnb-tagged style)

  1. Alien dictionary: derive a character ordering from a sorted list of words. [Reported] [4]
    • How do you detect an invalid ordering, such as a prefix appearing after a longer word?
  2. Palindrome pairs: find all index pairs whose concatenation forms a palindrome. [Reported] [4]
    • Can you do better than O(n^2 * k)?
  3. Cheapest flights within K stops (framed as a travel-itinerary cost minimizer). [Reported] [4]
    • What changes if edges carry both price and duration and you must optimize a weighted objective?
  4. Sliding puzzle: minimum moves to solve a 2x3 board. [Reported] [4]
  5. Pyramid transition: can you build a pyramid from a bottom row given allowed triples? [Reported] [4]
  6. Employee free time: given each person's busy intervals, return the common free intervals (calendar availability). [Reported] [4]
    • How would you adapt this to host calendars with blocked dates and minimum-night rules?
  7. Smallest common region: find the lowest common region in a region hierarchy. [Reported] [4]
  8. Combination sum (framed as "choose menu items that exactly use a budget"). [Reported] [4]
    • How do you handle float prices without precision bugs?
  9. Regular expression matching with . and *. [Reported] [4]
  10. Word search II on a Boggle-style board. [Reported] [4]
  11. Basic calculator with + - * / and parentheses. [Reported] [4]
  12. Contains duplicate within distance k and within value difference t. [Reported] [4]
  13. Flatten a nested list iterator / mini parser for nested lists. [Reported] [4]
  14. House robber (framed as non-adjacent booking selection maximizing revenue). [Reported] [4]
    • What if bookings are intervals with arbitrary overlaps (weighted interval scheduling)?

D. Onsite coding round 2 (practical, evolving requirements)

  1. Design and implement a booking calendar that supports book(start, end), rejects overlaps and answers is_available(start, end). [Likely] [3]
    • Now add per-night pricing and return the total price for a stay.
    • Now support split stays across two listings when no single listing is available.
  2. Implement a simple key-value store with transactions (begin, commit, rollback), nested. [Likely] [3]
  3. Parse a log of experiment exposures (user_id, experiment, variant, timestamp) and output per-variant unique user counts, de-duplicating users exposed to multiple variants. [Likely]
    • How would you flag and count users with conflicting assignments?
  4. Implement deterministic hash-based bucketing: given user_id, experiment salt and traffic split, return a variant. [Likely] [6]
    • How do you guarantee independence between experiments run on the same users?
    • How would you ramp traffic from 1% to 50% without reshuffling already-exposed users?
  5. Given streaming events, compute a rolling 7-day conversion rate per listing with bounded memory. [Likely]
  6. Implement a file system with create(path, value) and get(path), including parent-existence checks. [Reported] [4]
  7. Given guest reviews with ratings and timestamps, return the top-K listings by a time-decayed average rating. [Likely]
    • How do you handle listings with very few reviews (shrinkage)?
  8. Implement an LRU or LFU cache for search results, then extend it with a TTL. [Likely]
  9. Refactor your solution so that a new requirement (for example multi-currency support) fits in with minimal change. How do you structure the code? [Likely] [3]

E. ML design round (Airbnb product + experimentation)

  1. Design the search ranking system for Airbnb stays. [Likely] [7][8][9]
    • What are your labels (bookings, clicks, long clicks, host acceptances), and how do you weight them?
    • How do you handle position bias in training data?
    • How do you evaluate offline (NDCG on booked listings) versus online?
  2. Design real-time personalization using listing embeddings from in-session clicks. [Likely] [8]
    • How would you inject booking or market signals into the embedding objective?
    • How do you handle cold-start listings with no sessions?
  3. Design a dynamic pricing suggestion model for hosts (Smart Pricing). [Likely]
    • How do you estimate demand elasticity when price is confounded with seasonality and quality?
    • How do you A/B test a pricing model when hosts can override it?
  4. Design a fraud or risk model for bookings (Trust and Safety). [Likely]
    • How do you evaluate a model whose positive labels arrive weeks late and are censored by your own interventions?
  5. Design "similar listings" or "recommended experiences" recommendations. [Likely]
  6. Design a model that predicts host acceptance probability and use it in ranking. [Likely] [7]
  7. Design an ML system to detect low-quality or misleading listing photos and text. [Likely]
  8. How would you design the online experiment that launches your new ranking model? [Likely] [6][9]
    • What is the primary metric, and what are your guardrail metrics?
    • What unit of randomization would you use (user, session, listing, market), and why?
    • How do you estimate the required sample size and duration?
  9. In a two-sided marketplace, why can a user-randomized test of a ranking change be biased? [Likely] [6]
    • How would listing-side or market-level (cluster) randomization change your estimates?
    • What is interference, or a SUTVA violation, in this setting?
  10. When would you use interleaving instead of A/B testing for search ranking? [Likely] [9]
    • How does interleaving sensitivity compare with A/B, and what can interleaving not measure?
  11. Offline NDCG improved but the online A/B showed no booking lift. How do you debug this? [Likely] [7]
  12. Bookings happen weeks after search. How do you choose an experiment metric that is sensitive enough but still aligned with long-term value? [Likely] [6]
    • How would you build and validate a proxy or surrogate metric?
  13. How would you apply variance reduction (CUPED or regression adjustment) to booking-conversion experiments? [Likely] [6]
  14. How do you handle novelty effects and long-tail seasonality (holidays, events) when reading test results? [Likely]
  15. How do you detect and correct sample ratio mismatch? [Likely] [6]
  16. How do you control false discoveries when a team runs dozens of concurrent experiments and checks results daily? [Likely] [6]
    • What is the peeking problem, and how do sequential tests address it?
  17. How would you measure the long-term effect of a model change after the A/B test has ended (holdouts)? [Likely]
  18. Your model improves guest conversion but reduces host earnings in some markets. How do you decide whether to ship? [Likely]

F. System design round

  1. Design an experimentation assignment and logging service used by every Airbnb client (web, iOS, Android, backend). [Likely] [6]
    • How do you keep assignment consistent across logged-out and logged-in states?
    • How do you guarantee exposure logging is not lost or double-counted?
  2. Design the metrics computation pipeline for an experimentation platform (daily scorecards for thousands of metrics). [Likely] [6]
    • How do you backfill when a metric definition changes?
  3. Design a booking and reservation system that prevents double-booking under high concurrency. [Likely] [1]
  4. Design a feature store that serves both offline training and low-latency online ranking. [Likely]
    • How do you prevent training-serving skew?
  5. Design a search service that retrieves and ranks listings within 200 ms p99 across millions of listings. [Likely] [7]
    • Where do you place candidate retrieval, filtering (availability, price, map bounds) and ML re-ranking?
  6. Design a model serving and rollout system with shadow mode, canary and automatic rollback. [Likely]
  7. Design a notification system for hosts and guests that respects time zones and rate limits. [Likely]
  8. Design a calendar availability service for millions of listings with frequent updates and iCal sync. [Likely]

G. Past project deep dive

  1. Walk me through the ML project you are proudest of, from problem framing to measured impact. [Reported] [1][2]
    • What was your specific contribution compared with the team's?
    • What alternatives did you reject, and why?
  2. How did you measure the impact of that project? Was there an A/B test, and what did it show? [Likely]
    • What were the confidence intervals, and were there any surprising segment-level results?
  3. What was the hardest technical trade-off you made on that project? [Likely]
  4. If you had to redo the project with half the time, what would you cut? [Likely]
  5. Tell me about a model that worked offline but failed in production. What did you learn? [Likely]
  6. How did you get alignment from Product and Data Science on the success metric? [Likely]
  7. What would the next iteration of that system look like? [Likely]
  8. How did you handle data quality or labeling problems in that project? [Likely]
  9. Which part of the system would break first at 10x scale? [Likely]

H. Core values interviews (two rounds, cross-functional)

  1. Which of Airbnb's core values resonates with you most, and why? [Reported] [1][5]
  2. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer, user or teammate ("Be a Host"). [Reported] [1][5]

  5. What does "belonging" mean to you, and when have you created it for someone else ("Champion the Mission")? [Reported] [1][5]
  6. Describe a memorable travel or hosting experience and what it taught you. [Reported] [1]
  7. Tell me about a time you failed. What did you do next? [Reported] [1][2]
  8. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or manager. How did you resolve it? [Reported] [2]
  9. Tell me about a time you received difficult feedback. [Reported] [2]
  10. Describe a time you helped someone grow or mentored a junior colleague. [Likely]
  11. Tell me about a time you made a decision that was right for users but unpopular internally. [Likely]
  12. How do you build inclusive teams or include people with different perspectives? [Likely] [5]
  13. Tell me about a time you had to adapt to a big change you did not choose (a reorg, layoffs, a pivot). [Likely]
  14. What would your previous teammates say is the one thing you should improve? [Likely]
  15. Why do you want to work on a two-sided marketplace product? [Likely]

All of these are third-party, user-submitted material, so treat every [Reported] tag as "a candidate or aggregator says so", not as Airbnb confirmation. [Likely] marks an inference from the MLE/experimentation role. It does not mean anyone reported it.

Format notes

  • How many coding rounds an MLE gets. The one public MLE-track summary (3 candidate reports, mainly 2026 Q2) gives MLEs one technical phone screen and typically one onsite coding round (45-60 min). It says the track is "filtered by design depth, not by code": the decisive rounds are 1-2 ML system design rounds [1]. One 1point3acres MLE-type loop had one coding round (LC 1298) plus two ML design rounds (LTV and host pricing) [14]. Another had two ML design rounds plus a "ConnectN" OOD coding round [15]. SWE-track loops usually have 1-2 coding rounds plus a separate code review round (about 3 PRs) [2][4]. Ask your recruiter whether the MLE loop includes code review.
  • Runnable code with tests is the bar. Candidates report that Airbnb requires "bug free" code on the spot with all cases passing [20]. Other reports: code must run and be tested within 45 minutes [2]; there were "about 10 test cases" for Connect 4 [32]; problems arrived with stdin-style test strings [17]; one interviewer gave no interface, so the candidate had to agree the API and then write tests that pass [36]. Airbnb has long stressed "compilable, runnable code" [11]. One MLE screen expected the candidate to explain pruning or memoization before coding, then write test cases [1].
  • Shape of a round. A round is one practical problem in 45 min, often in two parts. Interviewers add constraints once part 1 runs, so leave 10-15 minutes for follow-ups [4][33]. Several posts say the interviewer said nothing and just pasted the problem [18][20]. Communication still counts: a candidate who stayed silent was marked down [12]. Switching algorithms mid-round and polishing helpers too early are cited as reasons for rejection [2].
  • Flavor. Problems are framed as product stories: split stays, group lodging, menus and combos, refunds, layovers, ski runs, host pagination, review highlighting. They test DP (often bitmask), graph search, simulation, parsing and small-object design rather than exotic tricks [1][3][4]. LeetCode's Airbnb tag over the last 6 months is led by Design Excel Sum Formula, Maximum Profit in Job Scheduling, Shortest Uncommon Substring, Maximum Candies from Boxes, Shopping Offers, Accounts Merge, Text Justification and Smallest Sufficient Team [5].
  • Senior bar. Seniors are expected to clarify ambiguous specs themselves, for example tie-breaking rules, whether the graph has cycles, and whether to return the path or only the cost [16]. They must also reach working code fast enough to discuss complexity and extensions. One candidate passed with a suboptimal solution that ran and passed every test, and only described the optimization [12][19]. Another failed because they could not beat O(N^2) when asked [19]. Several senior rejections were attributed to design or deep-dive rounds even when coding was strong [16][28].

Questions

A. Airbnb "classic" practical problems (long-running question bank)

  1. Display Page / pagination: given CSV search results (host_id, listing_id, score, city) sorted by score and a page size, reorder them so that a host appears at most once per page when possible, while otherwise preserving order. Print the pages as blocks. [Reported] [7][8][52]
    • What should happen when the remaining results all belong to one host?
    • Can you do it in one pass without repeatedly rescanning the list?
    • How would you test that the relative order inside a page is preserved?
  2. Text Justification: pack words greedily into lines of exactly width L, spreading extra spaces left-first, with the last line left-justified. [Reported] [5][7][8]
    • How do you handle a line that holds a single word?
    • What changes if words can be longer than L?
  3. Fixed-width text formatting (simpler variant: left-align and pad with trailing spaces), then lay the lines out into k side-by-side columns separated by one space. [Reported] [1][40][50]
    • When one column runs out of lines, what do you print in its place?
    • What is the complexity for n up to 1e5 words?
  4. Menu combination sum: given menu item prices, return every combination of items whose total is exactly the budget. Items may repeat, and duplicate combinations are not allowed. [Reported] [7][49]
    • Prices are floats like 2.40. How do you avoid floating-point equality bugs? [Reported] [17]
    • Now return only the count of combinations. Does order matter? [Reported] [10][48]
  5. Palindrome Pairs: find all pairs (i, j) such that words[i] + words[j] is a palindrome. [Reported] [5][7][39]
    • The interviewer wants O(nk) rather than O(nk^2). Which requirement do you clarify first? [Reported] [16]
    • How do the empty string and duplicate words affect correctness?
  6. IP range to CIDR: given a starting IPv4 address and a count n, return the shortest list of CIDR blocks that covers exactly that range. [Reported] [5][7][8]
    • How do you test the block boundaries, for example a range starting at an odd address?
  7. List-of-list (2D) iterator with hasNext, next and a remove that may be called once per next. [Reported] [5][7][8]
    • What should remove do when it is called twice, or before next?
    • How would you make the iterator lazy over a very large nested input?
  8. Flatten Nested List Iterator. [Reported] [5][6]
  9. Cheapest flights / minimum cost with at most K stops. Return both the cost and the route. [Reported] [5][7][27]
    • If the route must be returned, which algorithms still work?
    • How would you handle ties, or a cycle of zero-cost edges?
  10. Sliding puzzle: find the minimum moves to solve the 2x3 board (LC 773), or produce a move sequence for the 3x3 (8-puzzle) variant. [Reported] [5][7][8]
    • How do you detect that a board is unsolvable before searching?
    • Would A* help, and with which heuristic?
  11. Pour water / water drop: given terrain heights, pour V units at index K and return the final heights. [Reported] [5][7]
  12. Terrain rendering plus water simulation: Part 1 prints the terrain as ASCII from a heights array. Part 2 drops water at a column and prints the terrain with W for water. [Reported] [3][35]
    • Which way should water flow when both sides are equally low?
    • How do you test the printed output exactly, including trailing whitespace?
  13. Round prices: round each price up or down so that the rounded values sum to the rounded total, minimizing the total rounding error (LC 1058). [Reported] [5][7][10]
    • What do you return when the target is unreachable?
  14. CSV parser: parse a CSV line with quoted fields, escaped quotes and commas inside quotes. [Reported] [7][8]
    • How do you handle a quoted field that spans multiple lines?
  15. Design File System: create(path, value) fails if the parent is missing, and get(path) returns the value. [Reported] [5][7]
    • How would you add delete with recursive children?
  16. Alien Dictionary: derive the letter order from a sorted word list. [Reported] [5][6][8]
    • How do you detect an invalid input, such as a longer word appearing before its own prefix?

Format notes

  • Slot and length. Airbnb's loop is widely described as including one ML/ML-system-design round for MLE candidates. It runs about 45-60 minutes and is held over video, with a shared doc or whiteboard [13][14]. Chapter 01 of this guide covers the full loop.

  • Interviewer. Usually a senior or staff MLE from an ML-heavy area such as Search/Relevance, Pricing, Trust, Experiences/Recommendations or a platform team. Expect the interviewer to steer toward their own domain [Likely].

  • Shape of the round. Typically one open-ended Airbnb product prompt ("design search ranking", "design a fraud model"). You are expected to drive it: clarify the business goal, pick the objective and labels, design data and features, choose a model, plan offline evaluation and online experimentation, cover serving and latency, and plan monitoring and iteration [Likely; 1][2].

  • Senior bar (L5). Signals that the round assesses:

    • You frame the problem as a marketplace problem: guest and host, supply and demand.
    • You choose labels and objectives with care, including delayed or censored outcomes.
    • You propose a pragmatic baseline before deep models.
    • You reason about position bias, feedback loops and interference.
    • You design the launch experiment yourself. This matters especially for an experimentation-flavored role.
    • You discuss trade-offs without heavy prompting.

    Airbnb's own search papers emphasize simple baselines, failed ideas and lessons learned. Expect interviewers to reward the same humility and iteration [Likely; 1][2].

  • Experimentation weight. For this role, assume every design will end with "how would you launch and measure it?" Expect probes on interleaving, guardrails, two-sided interference and long-horizon metrics [Likely; 8][9][17].

Questions

A. Search ranking for stays (core prompt)

  1. Design the search ranking system for Airbnb stays: a guest enters a location, dates and guest count, and you return an ordered list of listings. [Likely] [1][2]
    • What is the business objective: bookings, booking value, guest satisfaction or long-term host and guest retention? How do you encode it?
    • What are the stages (candidate retrieval, filtering, first-pass ranker, second-pass re-ranker), and what is each stage's latency budget?
  2. What label would you train on, given that bookings are rare and many searches end with no booking? [Likely] [1]
    • How would you use clicks, listing-page views, wishlist saves, contacts to host and bookings as weaker labels or auxiliary tasks?
    • How do you treat a booking request the host later rejected or cancelled?
  3. How would you frame ranking: pointwise, pairwise or listwise? Why? [Likely] [1][2]
    • How would you build pairs from one search session with a single booked listing?
  4. How do you correct for position bias in logged search data? [Likely] [2]
    • How would you model position as a feature during training, and what do you set it to at inference time?
    • How would you validate that your debiasing helped, rather than just shifting the distribution?
  5. Which feature families would you build for listings, queries, guests and query-listing pairs? [Likely] [1]
    • How do you normalize price so that the model generalizes across markets and currencies?
    • How do you represent location relevance (distance to map center, the neighborhood the guest wanted)?
  6. Your first model was a GBDT. When would you move to a neural network, and what would you expect to break? [Likely] [1]
    • How do you handle feature scaling, sparse categorical features and very wide value ranges in a neural ranker?
  7. The ranker strongly prefers cheaper listings. Is that a bug or correct behavior? How would you investigate? [Likely] [1][2]
    • How would you make the model price-aware without hard-coding a monotonic price penalty?
  8. How do you handle cold-start listings that have no booking or review history? [Likely] [2]
    • How do you estimate engagement for a new listing, and how do you give it controlled exposure without hurting conversion?
  9. How would you add diversity to the results so the top of the page is not ten near-identical listings? [Likely] [5]
    • How would you measure diversity, and how would you trade it off against relevance in the loss?
  10. How would you rank listings shown as map pins, where there is no clear top-to-bottom position? [Likely] [2]
    • How does user attention on a map differ from attention on a list, and how does that change your discounting?
  11. How would you model the whole guest search journey (many searches across days before one booking) rather than a single search? [Likely] [6]
    • What would a multi-task model with heads for clicks, long views, wishlist and booking look like? How do you weight the heads?
  12. How do you handle flexible-date or flexible-location searches ("I'm flexible", category browsing)? [Likely] [11]
  13. How would you incorporate host-side outcomes (acceptance, cancellations, host earnings) into guest-facing ranking? [Likely] [1]
  14. Offline NDCG went up but the online A/B test showed flat bookings. Walk me through your debugging plan. [Likely] [1][2]
    • How would you check for training-serving skew, feature leakage and a mismatch between offline and online candidate sets?
  15. What offline evaluation set would you build, and how do you avoid leakage from future data or from repeat sessions by the same guest? [Likely] [1]

B. Candidate retrieval and serving infrastructure for search

  1. Design candidate retrieval that cuts millions of listings to a few thousand within strict latency, respecting date availability, guest count and map bounds. [Likely] [12]
    • Where would embedding-based retrieval fit next to hard filters, and how do you combine them?
  2. How would you train a two-tower model for embedding-based retrieval of listings? [Likely] [12]
    • What is your negative-sampling strategy (in-batch, same-market, hard negatives)?
    • How do you handle availability, which changes daily and cannot easily live inside the embedding?
  3. How would you serve a neural ranker at p99 latency under 200 ms for thousands of candidates per query? [Likely]
    • Which features are precomputed offline, which are computed in real time, and how do you keep them consistent?
  4. How do you prevent training-serving skew in features such as "listing price for these dates" or "number of recent bookings"? [Likely] [1]
  5. How often would you retrain the ranking model, and how would you detect that it has gone stale? [Likely]
  6. What would you log at serving time so you can retrain, debias and debug later? [Likely] [2]

C. Listing embeddings and real-time personalization

  1. Design listing embeddings trained from guest click sessions, and use them for similar-listing recommendations and in-session personalization. [Likely] [3][10]
    • How do you define a session and its context window?
    • What negatives would you use? Why might random negatives make embeddings that only learn geography?
  2. How would you change the embedding objective so that the booked listing influences every listing in the session? [Likely] [3]
  3. How would you build user-type and listing-type embeddings to capture long-term preferences from sparse booking histories? [Likely] [3]
  4. How would you create an embedding for a brand-new listing with no sessions? [Likely] [3]
  5. How would you evaluate embeddings offline before using them in production? [Likely] [3]
    • What would a "rank of the eventually booked listing among the clicked listings" evaluation look like?
  6. How would you turn in-session clicks and skips into real-time ranking features? [Likely] [3]
    • What streaming infrastructure is needed, and what is the freshness requirement?
  7. When would you replace skip-gram embeddings with a sequence model or transformer over user actions? What would you expect to gain? [Likely]

D. Pricing (host price suggestions / Smart Pricing)

  1. Design a system that suggests a nightly price to hosts for each future date. [Likely] [4][16]
    • What is the objective: booking probability, expected host revenue, or marketplace occupancy?
    • Why can't you train directly on "the optimal price" as a label?
  2. How would you predict booking probability as a function of price, lead time and listing quality? [Likely] [4]
    • How do you handle price being confounded with demand, because hosts raise prices when they expect demand?
  3. How would you estimate price elasticity per listing or per market from observational data? [Likely] [4]
    • When would you need randomized price experiments, and what ethical and host-trust constraints apply?
  4. Hosts can accept, modify or ignore your suggestion. How do you evaluate the model offline and online? [Likely] [4]
    • What is your unit of randomization, and how do you handle spillover between treated and control hosts in the same market?
  5. How would you forecast market demand for a city on a given date (events, holidays, seasonality)? [Likely] [16]
  6. How would you prevent the pricing model from producing extreme or volatile suggestions that destroy host trust? [Likely]
  7. How would you design total-price display or fee-related ranking changes, and how would you measure whether guests trust the price more? [Likely]
  8. A pricing-model launch raised bookings but cut average host revenue. How do you decide? [Likely] [17]

E. Fraud, risk and trust and safety

  1. Design a model that scores every booking for payment-fraud risk in real time. [Likely] [18]
    • Chargeback labels arrive weeks later. How do you train and evaluate with delayed labels?
    • How do you handle label censoring, where blocked transactions never reveal whether they were fraud?
  2. Rather than block or allow, how would you design graduated friction (ID verification, extra payment checks) driven by risk scores? [Likely] [18]
    • How do you choose thresholds per action, given the cost of false positives (lost good guests) and false negatives?
  3. Design an account-takeover detection system for guest and host logins. [Likely] [18]
  4. Design a system that predicts and prevents unauthorized parties at listings. [Likely] [19]
    • What features are legitimate and fair to use, and which risk proxy discrimination (for example by age)?
    • How do you measure the system's impact when the outcome (a party happened) is rarely observed?
  5. Design a fake or scam listing detector at listing-creation time. [Likely] [19]
    • How do you combine text, image, host-history and graph signals (shared devices, payment instruments)?
  6. How would you use graph-based features or graph neural networks to detect fraud rings? [Likely]
  7. Fraudsters adapt to your model within weeks. How do you design for adversarial drift? [Likely]
    • How do you keep a clean holdout to measure true fraud rates when the model blocks most of it?
  8. How do you A/B test a fraud model when exposing the control group to fraud has real costs? [Likely] [18]
  9. How do you build fairness checks into a trust model that affects who can book? [Likely] [19]

F. Recommendations (Experiences, similar listings, discovery surfaces)

  1. Design search ranking for Airbnb Experiences, which has far less data than stays. [Likely] [7]
    • How would you stage the system as data grows (heuristics, then GBDT, then personalization, then online scoring)?
  2. Design "similar listings" recommendations on the listing detail page. [Likely] [3]
    • What is the right objective: clicks, bookings, or rescuing guests whose first choice is unavailable?
  3. Design homepage recommendations for a logged-in guest with no active search ("where should I go next?"). [Likely]
  4. Design personalized marketing or email recommendations (destinations, wishlisted listings with price drops). [Likely]
    • How do you measure incremental lift rather than attribution from people who would have booked anyway?
  5. Design recommendations for cross-selling Experiences or Services to guests with an upcoming stay. [Likely]
  6. How would you recommend wishlist listings or destinations for group trips where several people collaborate? [Likely]
  7. How do you avoid a popularity feedback loop in which top listings get more exposure, more bookings and more exposure? [Likely] [5]

G. Host acceptance, response time and other time-to-event predictions

  1. Design a model that predicts the probability a host will accept a booking request, and use it in ranking. [Likely] [1]
    • How do you avoid unfairly penalizing a host for a single rejection?
    • How do you use host preferences (trip length, lead time, gaps between bookings) as features?
  2. Design a model that predicts host response time to a guest message or booking request. [Likely]
    • How do you handle censoring for messages that never get a reply?
  3. Predict the probability that a confirmed booking will be cancelled by the host or by the guest. [Likely]
    • How would you use this in ranking, in host coaching, or in guest-protection policy?
  4. Predict booking lead time and length of stay for a search, and explain how they would change ranking. [Likely] [1]
  5. Design a model that estimates customer-support wait time or resolution time, and routes cases to the right agent queue. [Likely] [20]
  6. Which model families would you consider for time-to-event problems (survival models, hazard models, classification at fixed horizons)? Why? [Likely]

H. Categories, content understanding and listing quality

  1. Design the system behind Airbnb Categories (for example "Beachfront", "Treehouses"), which assigns listings to themed browse categories. [Likely] [11]
    • How do you combine ML candidates with human-in-the-loop review, and how do you budget human review?
    • How do you rank listings within a category, and how do you rank the categories themselves for a user?
  2. Design an image-understanding pipeline that detects amenities and room types from listing photos. [Likely] [21]
    • How would you get labels cheaply, and how do you measure precision when amenities drive search filters?
  3. How would you choose and order a listing's cover photo to maximize engagement without misleading guests? [Likely]
  4. Design a listing-quality score used in ranking and host coaching (photos, description, completeness, reviews). [Likely]
    • How do you keep the score from simply rewarding listings that are already popular?
  5. Design a system that detects inaccurate or misleading listing descriptions by comparing text, photos and reviews. [Likely]
  6. How would you use LLMs or multimodal models for listing understanding at Airbnb's scale while controlling cost? [Likely]
  7. How would you translate or localize listing content and decide when machine translation is good enough to show? [Likely]

I. Reviews: quality, ranking and trust

  1. Design a system that ranks reviews on a listing page so guests see the most helpful reviews first. [Likely]
    • How do you define "helpful" without explicit helpfulness votes?
  2. Design a fake or retaliatory review detector. [Likely] [19]
  3. Design a system that extracts aspects (cleanliness, location, accuracy) from review text and feeds them into search or listing quality. [Likely]
  4. Design LLM-generated review summaries or highlights for a listing. [Likely]
    • How do you evaluate faithfulness, so the summary claims nothing the reviews do not support?
    • How do you A/B test whether summaries change booking or cancellation behavior?
  5. Ratings are highly skewed toward 5 stars. How do you build a reliable quality signal from them? [Likely]
    • How would you apply Bayesian shrinkage for listings with few reviews?
  6. How would you predict a guest's post-stay satisfaction before the stay, and how would you use it in ranking? [Likely] [1]

J. Messaging, customer support and LLM systems

  1. Design an ML system that classifies the intent of guest-host messages to route or auto-suggest replies. [Likely] [20]
  2. Design an LLM-based customer-support assistant that resolves common issues (cancellations, refunds) and escalates the rest. [Likely] [20]
    • How do you evaluate it offline, monitor hallucinations and policy errors, and measure deflection versus satisfaction online?
    • What guardrails are needed for actions involving money or safety?
  3. Design suggested replies for hosts. How do you avoid generating replies that commit the host to things they did not intend? [Likely]
  4. How would you build retrieval-augmented generation over help-center articles and booking context? [Likely]
  5. How would you decide whether to fine-tune, prompt or distill a model for a high-volume support classifier? [Likely]

K. Experimentation and measurement inside the ML design round

  1. For your ranking model, what is the primary metric of the launch A/B test, and what are the guardrails? [Likely] [8][17]
    • Why might "bookings" be a better primary metric than "booking requests" or "clicks"?
  2. What randomization unit would you use for a search ranking test (guest, session, search, listing, market)? Why? [Likely] [8]
  3. Explain why a guest-randomized test of a ranking change can be biased in a two-sided marketplace with limited inventory. [Likely] [8]
    • How could market-level, cluster or switchback designs help? What do they cost in statistical power?
  4. When would you use interleaving instead of a classic A/B test for ranking? [Likely] [9]
    • How do you attribute a booking to one ranker in an interleaved result list?
    • How do you validate that interleaving and A/B testing agree on direction?
  5. Bookings happen days after search, and stays happen weeks later. How do you get a sensitive, timely decision metric? [Likely] [8][17]
    • How would you build and validate a surrogate or predicted long-term value metric?
  6. How would you reduce variance in a booking-conversion experiment (CUPED, stratification, trimming)? [Likely] [8]
  7. How do you detect and handle sample ratio mismatch, bot traffic or logging bugs in a ranking experiment? [Likely] [8]
  8. How do you handle novelty and primacy effects, and seasonality around holidays, in experiment readouts? [Likely]
  9. Your model wins overall but loses badly in one market or one guest segment. What do you do? [Likely]
  10. How would you set up a long-term holdout to measure the cumulative impact of many ranking launches? [Likely]
  11. How would you measure the impact of a pricing or fraud model where the treatment changes host or fraudster behavior over time? [Likely] [4][18]
  12. How would you use counterfactual or off-policy evaluation from logged ranking data before running an online test? [Likely] [2]
    • What propensities do you need to log, and how much randomization would you inject?
  13. How do you trade off guest-side and host-side metrics in a single ship decision? [Likely] [17]

L. Monitoring, reliability and iteration (senior depth)

  1. What would you monitor once your ranking or fraud model is live (data drift, feature nulls, score distributions, business KPIs)? [Likely]
    • Which alerts would page an on-call engineer, and which go to a dashboard?
  2. How would you roll out a new model safely (shadow scoring, canary, ramp, automatic rollback)? [Likely]
  3. A feature pipeline broke silently and the model has served on stale features for three days. How do you detect it, mitigate it and prevent it from happening again? [Likely]
  4. How do you decide what to build next after launch: more features, a bigger model, or better labels? [Likely] [1][2]
  5. How would you reduce serving cost by 50% without measurable loss in bookings? [Likely]
    • What is your approach to distillation, caching and candidate-set pruning?
  6. How would you design the system so that other teams (Experiences, Services, Long-term stays) can reuse your ranking stack? [Likely]
  7. How would you explain to a product manager why a model change that "looks worse" in qualitative review should still ship? [Likely]
  8. Which parts of this design would you not use ML for at all, and why? [Likely] [1]
  9. What are the main fairness and discrimination risks in Airbnb ranking and trust systems, and how would you audit for them? [Likely] [19]

M. Variations and curveball prompts

  1. Design ranking for long-term stays (28+ nights). What changes from nightly stays? [Likely]
  2. Design ranking for Airbnb Services or new verticals with almost no historical data. [Likely] [7]
  3. Design autocomplete and query understanding for the search box (locations, landmarks, categories). [Likely]
  4. Design a model that predicts which markets will face supply shortages for an upcoming event, and recommend actions to recruit hosts. [Likely]
  5. Design a host-onboarding recommendation system that tells new hosts what to improve first. [Likely]
  6. Design a system that detects duplicate listings (the same property listed several times or across accounts). [Likely]
  7. Design a "price drop" or "rare find" badge. How would you verify that badges are accurate and actually help guests? [Likely]

Format notes

  • Where this material shows up. Public descriptions of Airbnb's engineering loop (coding x2, system design, ML design, past-project deep dive, core values x2) do not list a separate "A/B testing" round for MLEs [9][10]. For an MLE role that names experimentation or A/B testing, expect it in three places: (a) the evaluation and launch half of the ML design round; (b) the past-project deep dive, with questions like "how did you know it worked?"; and (c) possibly a dedicated experimentation or analytics conversation with a data scientist or experimentation-platform engineer. Ask your recruiter which of these your loop includes. [Likely]

  • Duration and interviewer. Usually 45-60 minutes with a senior or staff MLE, a data scientist (Airbnb's DS org has an Inference/Experimentation track) or an engineer from the experimentation platform team (ERF) [1][3]. [Likely]

  • The senior (L5) bar. It goes beyond knowing the t-test. Expect to show you can:

    • spot when the standard A/B setup is biased (interference, novelty, selection, peeking);
    • choose the randomization unit and the metrics, and defend that choice;
    • quantify trade-offs between bias and variance and between speed and rigor;
    • turn ambiguous or conflicting results into a launch decision that Product and leadership accept.

    Airbnb's own writing stresses that experiments run on a two-sided marketplace with long, rare conversion funnels (search, then contact or book, then stay, then review). Expect probes on exactly that [1][2][4][5]. [Likely]

  • Format of the questions. Expect open-ended scenarios ("we changed X in search ranking; design the test; results came back like this; what now?"). Some rounds may add light whiteboard math, such as sample size, standard errors or CUPED adjustment. Expect few closed-form trivia questions. [Likely]

Questions

A. Airbnb experimentation platform (ERF) and experiment fundamentals

  1. Walk me through how you would design an online A/B test for a new search-ranking model at Airbnb, from hypothesis to launch decision. [Likely; 1][2]
    • What is your primary metric, and why not just click-through rate?
    • What is the randomization unit, and what are you assuming by choosing it?
    • How long would you run it, and what determines that?
  2. Airbnb's experimentation platform (ERF, the Experiment Reporting Framework) computes metrics for hundreds of concurrent experiments. If you were designing such a platform, what are its core components? [Likely; 3]
    • How would you implement deterministic, salted hashing for assignment so that concurrent experiments are independent?
    • How do you log exposures versus assignments, and why does the difference matter?
    • How would you make metric definitions consistent across teams (for example, through a central metrics layer)? [Likely; 11]
  3. What is the difference between assignment and exposure (trigger) analysis, and when is an intent-to-treat analysis the right choice? [Likely]
    • The treatment only changes the page when a listing has more than 10 photos. Who should be in your analysis population?
    • How does triggering affect your power, and how do you translate a triggered effect back to a site-wide impact?
  4. Many Airbnb users browse logged out and on several devices before booking. How does this affect randomization and metric attribution? [Likely; 1]
    • Would you randomize by cookie, device, user ID or something else? What breaks in each case?
    • How would you handle a user who is assigned on a logged-out device and then logs in?
  5. What is a sample ratio mismatch (SRM)? How would you detect it, and what are its common causes on a web/mobile booking site? [Likely]
    • The treatment loads more slowly, so fewer treatment users fire the exposure log. What does that do to your results?

The fetched candidate-report summary says no reported round asked for derivations, loss-function proofs or architecture comparisons. "Paper-level theory" and naming a fashionable model without a metric are listed as not tested [1].
  • Where fundamentals actually show up: as depth probes inside the ML system design round (one or two rounds, 45-60 min each, run by senior/staff MLEs, described as the core and decisive round of the track). They also show up in the experience / project deep dive (45-60 min, a single past project). ML design prompts are sometimes folded into the 45-60 min technical phone screen too [1].
  • Expected structure in those rounds: framing → label definition → data sources → features → model choice → offline evaluation → serving, latency, freshness, cold start for new listings → experimentation. Model choice took little time in the reported rounds. Jumping straight to the model is the most-cited failure [1].
  • Recurring reported follow-up themes: turning an offline metric into an online one, spatial features such as geohashing, and framing a target as regression vs. bucketized classification. In the lifetime-value prompt the interviewer pushed toward bucketized classification [1].
  • Senior (L5) bar: the problem scope you owned matters more than model sophistication [1]. Expect "why" questions on every choice: why this label, why GBDT or NN, why this metric, and what you would expect to move online. Questions in this chapter are written as those depth probes. Practise answering each one in 2-4 minutes, tied to an Airbnb surface.
  • Airbnb context that interviewers know well: search moved from GBDT to deep NNs [5][6]. In-session listing embeddings [7][8], multi-objective journey ranking [10], learning to rank diversely [9], a customized pricing regression loss [11] and interleaving for ranking experiments [12] are all public. 06. General Backend System Design: Booking, Availability, Search, Messaging, Payments and Feature Store

Research note. This session's web-search budget ran out, and most interview sites (Glassdoor, Blind, LeetCode, Reddit, Medium, IGotAnOffer, TrueInterview, the Airbnb Tech Blog) could not be reached. The chapter therefore rests on the sources that did load: aggregated candidate reports for 2025 to 2026 hosted on GitHub [1][2][3][4], a community prep guide [5], a crowd-sourced question list [6], the Tech Interview Handbook [7] and Airbnb's open-source Chronon repository [8]. Every other question is [Likely]: an inference from Airbnb's products and the senior bar described in those sources. Before the onsite, re-run searches on Glassdoor, Blind and LeetCode Discuss for "Airbnb system design 2026".

Format notes

  • Rounds and length. Candidates report one or two system design rounds of 45 to 60 minutes each. A second design round is sometimes added late in the process when the signal is borderline, and one candidate said that extra round decided the outcome [1][2]. The Tech Interview Handbook lists one "System Design/architecture" round in the general engineering onsite [7]. For the 1-to-3-year level (G8), one report describes a 60-minute design screen plus a 60-minute onsite design round on Miro or Excalidraw [5].
  • Interviewer. A senior or staff engineer runs the round. MLE loops usually have a separate ML system design round (see chapter 03), but generic backend prompts also appear in engineering loops at senior level [1][2][3].
  • Senior bar. The aggregated reports say design depth "reads staff-level even for senior candidates." Candidates are expected to "drive requirements yourself, fix a minimal scope in the first few minutes, then go deep where the interviewer leans in" [1].
  • Common failure. Trying to cover every feature. One candidate lost an in-memory SQL engine round by attempting the whole feature set without first agreeing what "done" meant. The reports say design rounds "reward scoping and negotiated trade-offs" [1].
  • Recurring themes. Double-booking prevention, availability search, and hot partitions caused by a big event [1][5]. Others are notification fan-out, chat, job scheduling, key-value stores pushed onto CAP trade-offs, and streaming aggregation with Flink and Kafka [1][3][4][6].
  • What this means for an MLE. Expect a classic backend prompt framed around Airbnb's own products (booking, calendar, search, messaging). Expect the interviewer to probe whatever your background makes natural: feature stores, logging pipelines and experiment assignment.

  5. How would you build the search index (inverted index plus geo index) and keep it fresh as listings, prices and availability change? [Likely]
    • Full reindex versus incremental updates: what triggers each?
  6. Design autocomplete for destinations and points of interest. [Likely]
  7. Design semantic search over user and listing embeddings. [Reported] [3]
    • How do you choose and operate an approximate nearest-neighbor index (HNSW, IVF-PQ) next to structured filters?
    • How do you re-embed and re-index without downtime?
  8. Design the host listings page, which aggregates data from many services for hosts with hundreds of listings, and optimize it. [Reported] [3]
    • What would you pre-compute, cache or paginate?
    • How do you avoid N+1 calls across service boundaries?
  9. Design pagination for search results that stays stable when ranking or availability changes between page loads. [Likely]
  10. How would you cache search results, given that the same location and dates are queried by many users but prices and availability change? [Likely]
  11. Design the wishlist service, including shared wishlists among group members. [Likely]

D. Messaging and notifications

  1. Design a group chat system (WhatsApp-like). [Reported] [5]

  2. Design group chat with a feature that returns a user's top-N most active chats. [Reported] [1]
    • How would you define and maintain "active" in real time?
  3. Design a Slack-like chat system. [Reported] [3][4]
  4. Design guest-host messaging on Airbnb: messages tied to a listing or reservation, with read receipts and translation. [Likely]
    • How do you store the conversation so it is linked to the reservation and visible to support agents?
    • Where would you hook in fraud or off-platform-payment detection without adding latency to sending?
  5. Design a unified notification platform that handles both event-triggered and batch sends. [Reported] [1][3][4]
    • How do you prioritize transactional notifications (booking confirmed) over marketing?
    • How do you de-duplicate, rate-limit per user and respect quiet hours across time zones?
    • What happens when a downstream provider (APNs, FCM, an SMS gateway) is slow or failing?
  6. How would you design per-user notification preferences and channel fallbacks (push, then email, then SMS)? [Likely]
  7. Design a customer-support ticketing system that routes tickets to the right agent queue. [Reported] [3]
    • How do you combine rules-based routing with an ML classifier, and how do you fall back when the model is down?
  8. Design a system that generates customer-support instructions from company policies, for example with an LLM. [Reported] [3]
    • How do you keep generated instructions in sync when a policy changes?
    • How do you evaluate correctness before showing them to agents?
  9. Design a system that sends reminder messages (check-in instructions, review requests) at specific times relative to each reservation. [Likely]

  1. How would you design the payment flow so a guest is never charged twice, even with retries and partial failures? [Likely] [9]

  2. Design host payouts: scheduling, splitting among co-hosts, multiple currencies and payout methods. [Likely]
  3. Design refunds across multiple payment methods and dates, honoring a priority order. [Likely] [10]
  4. Design a ledger (double-entry) for Airbnb's payments platform. [Likely]
    • How do you reconcile your ledger against the payment processor's settlement files?
  5. Design a banking-like service with deposit, withdraw, transfer and balance. Follow-up: how would you scale it into a distributed service? [Likely] [1]
  6. Design a currency-conversion and price-display service that shows consistent prices from search through checkout. [Likely]
    • How do you lock an exchange rate for a quote?
  7. Design a retryer or retry library for calls to unreliable downstream services. [Likely] [1]
    • How do you decide which errors are retryable?

  8. How would you design fraud checks into the payment path without hurting checkout latency? [Likely]

  2. Design an ad-click (or listing-click) event aggregation system. [Reported] [3]

  3. Model cumulative booking metrics, including the first and last booking times per user or listing. [Reported] [3]

  4. Design the logging pipeline that captures search impressions, clicks and bookings for ranking training data. [Likely]

  5. Design a metrics and dashboard system for marketplace health (bookings, nights, supply) with daily and real-time views. [Likely]
  6. How would you design data-quality checks and alerting for core tables that feed models and experiments? [Likely]

G. Feature store and ML platform infrastructure

  1. Design a feature store that serves offline training and low-latency online ranking from one feature definition. [Likely] [8]

  2. How would you support windowed aggregations (for example a listing's bookings in the last 7 days) in both streaming and batch? [Likely] [8]

  3. Design the online serving layer of a feature store for a ranking request that fetches features for 1,000 candidate listings in under 50 ms. [Likely] [8]

  4. Design the metrics pipeline that computes experiment scorecards daily for hundreds of experiments and thousands of metrics. [Likely]
  5. How would you design a kill switch or automated guardrail that stops an experiment when a key metric degrades? [Likely]
  6. Design a network-aware experiment for a "Wi-Fi badge" on listings where treatment effects can spill over between listings. [Reported] [3]

Research note (read first). The shared web-search budget was already used up when this chapter was written, so no new search queries could be run. Tags: [Reported] means a cited source says a candidate was asked this, or something very close to it, at Airbnb. [Likely] means an inference from the role, the senior bar and Airbnb's products. It does not mean anyone reported it. Most of the deep-dive reports come from SWE and infrastructure loops, but the MLE guide says the MLE track runs the same round format [1].

Format notes

Aspect What sources report

Length 45-60 min. One candidate's round was booked for 1 hour and ran about 90 minutes [1][8][9][14]
Interviewer Usually a senior IC or an engineering manager, and sometimes a senior manager from a neighbouring team (staff loops) [16][21][25]. A separate hiring-manager (HM) call often repeats the deep dive and adds behavioral questions [9][22][23]
Shape One project, taken apart: architecture, the decisions that were yours, trade-offs, measured impact. Interviewers keep asking "until they heard what they wanted to hear" [1][6][16]. In at least one loop the deep dive and a coding exercise shared a single session [26]. Behavioral and values questions are scored in separate rounds run by employees from any function [5]
Senior / L5 bar The perceived scope of your work is scored on its own. Lead with problem size, ownership and outcome, not model details [1]. The round "also drives leveling" [2]. Scope judgements here can overturn strong technical rounds [2][3]
Weight One recruiter told a candidate "the deep dive round is the most important", and Airbnb would not re-run it [18]. Several senior rejections were pinned on this round alone [8][18][20]

Prep advice from reports Know every line on your resume: one candidate was grilled on a project that took one line [12]. Bring metrics, a conflict and a rejected alternative [2][3]. At staff level, compare your system with how Airbnb solved a similar problem on its tech blog, and say why you chose your approach [24]

How to use this chapter: choose one flagship ML project that touched an experiment or online metric, with clear cross-team reach. Practise a 10-minute arc, then drill every question below against that project. Prepare a backup project for questions that ask for a different story, such as a failure or a conflict.

Questions

A. Opening, project selection and the 10-minute arc

  1. Walk me through the project you are proudest of. [Reported] [16][11][29]
    • Why this project and not another one on your resume?
    • Can you give me the two-minute version before we go deep?
  2. Pick one recent project and walk me through it from start to finish. [Reported] [19][25]
    • Where do you want to start: the problem, the system or the result?
  3. Tell me about the most interesting project you have worked on. [Reported] [22]
  4. Show me a project you are proud of that was cross-organizational or cross-functional. [Reported] [11]
    • Which teams outside yours had to change something for it to ship?
  5. Walk me through the projects on your resume. What were you responsible for on each? [Reported] [13]
    • You wrote one line about this older project. Tell me about it in detail. [Reported] [12]
  6. Tell me about the ML project with the biggest business impact that you led end to end. [Likely] [1]
    • Who used its output, and how many users, listings or dollars did it touch?
  7. If you had to describe the project's scope in one sentence (problem size, your ownership, outcome), what would it be? [Likely] [1]
  8. Can you draw the overall system on the whiteboard while you explain? [Reported] [14][6]

B. Problem framing and business context

  1. What problem was the project solving, and why did it matter to the business at that time? [Reported] [17][6]
    • What would have happened if nobody had done this project?
  2. What decision or product surface did the model's output feed into? [Likely] [1]
    • Who consumed the score: a ranker, a rule engine, an ops team or a user-facing UI?
  3. How did you turn a vague product goal into a measurable prediction problem? [Likely] [1]
    • What was the label, and why that label rather than a nearby proxy?
  4. What was the scale: traffic, QPS, data volume, number of entities? [Reported] [10]
    • What about that scale made the problem hard?
  5. Why did you split the work into a short-term plan and a long-term plan? Why was the long-term plan needed at all? [Reported] [10]
  6. Was ML actually necessary here? What was the heuristic or rules baseline? [Likely]
    • By how much did ML beat the baseline, and was the extra complexity worth it?
  7. How did you size the opportunity before you started? [Likely]

C. Ownership: what was yours versus the team's

  1. What was your specific role on the project? [Reported] [15][13]
    • Which decisions did you personally make, and which did you inherit?
  2. How was the work divided across the team? [Reported] [9]
    • Who else was on the project, and what did each person own?
  3. Did you lead the project, or contribute to it? How many people did you direct or influence? [Likely] [1]
  4. Were the metrics you analysed already there, or did you add them? [Reported] [10]
  5. What did you do that nobody asked you to do? [Likely]
  6. If you had left halfway through, what would not have happened? [Likely]

D. Data, labels and features

  1. Where did the training data come from, and what were its biggest quality problems? [Likely] [1]
    • How did you detect and handle label leakage?
    • How did you handle delayed or censored labels (for example a booking that happens weeks after a search)?
  2. How did you build the label, and how noisy was it? [Likely] [1]
    • Did you consider regression versus a bucketed classification framing? [Likely] [1]
  3. Which features mattered most, and how do you know? [Likely]
    • Did any "obviously useful" feature turn out to hurt?
  4. How did you keep training and serving features consistent? [Likely]
    • Did you ever ship with training-serving skew? How was it caught?
  5. How did you handle cold start for new users or new items? [Likely] [1]
  6. Did you use spatial or geographic signals? How did you encode them? [Likely] [1]
  7. Did your training data come from logs produced by an earlier model? How did you deal with feedback-loop and position bias? [Likely] [30]

E. Modeling decisions and trade-offs

  1. Walk me through the architecture and the key design points. [Reported] [6][23]
    • Why this design over the obvious alternative?
  2. What were the main trade-offs you made? [Reported] [9][21]
    • What did you give up, and how did you decide it was acceptable?
  3. What alternatives did you consider and reject? [Reported] [2][3]
    • What evidence made you reject each one?
  4. Why this model family (for example GBDT versus a neural network)? [Likely] [1][30]
    • When did you decide to move to something more complex, and what convinced you?
  5. What was the single modeling change that gave the biggest gain? [Likely]
    • What did you try that did not work? [Likely] [30]
  6. How did you tune the model, and how did you avoid overfitting to your validation set? [Likely]
  7. How did you trade model quality against latency, cost or interpretability? [Likely]
  8. If the interviewer cannot follow your domain, how would you simplify the core idea in two minutes? [Reported] [8][14]

F. Architecture, serving and production

  1. Walk me through the implementation details end to end, from data pipeline to serving. [Reported] [6]
  2. What were the latency and throughput requirements at serving time, and how did you meet them? [Likely]
  3. Was inference batch, online or both? Why? [Likely]
  4. How did you deploy new model versions safely (shadow, canary, rollback)? [Likely]
  5. How did you make sure of system quality after deployment? [Reported] [17]
    • What monitoring and alerting did you set up for data drift and model decay?
  6. What broke in production, and how did you find out? [Likely]
  7. Which part of the system would break first at 10x scale? [Likely]
  8. Did you build or reuse a platform (feature store, training pipeline, experiment framework)? Why? [Likely]

G. Offline-to-online evaluation and experimentation (A/B testing focus)

  1. How did you evaluate the model offline, and which metric did you trust? [Likely] [1]
    • How well did the offline metric predict the online result?
  2. How did an offline metric gain become an online metric gain? [Likely] [1]
  3. How did you know it worked? Was there an A/B test? [Likely] [31]
    • What was the primary metric, and what were the guardrails?
    • What was the randomization unit, and why?
    • How long did it run, and how did you choose the sample size?
  4. What were the confidence intervals on the main result? Were any segments surprising? [Likely]
  5. Did offline gains ever fail to show up online? How did you debug it? [Likely] [30]
  6. Did your experiment face interference effects (for example a two-sided marketplace or shared inventory)? How did you handle it? [Likely] [31]
  7. Did you consider faster methods such as interleaving for a ranking change? Why or why not? [Likely] [32]
  8. Did you use variance reduction such as CUPED? What did it buy you? [Likely]
  9. How did you handle novelty effects, seasonality or ramp-up effects when you read the result? [Likely]
  10. Did you check for sample ratio mismatch or logging bugs before you trusted the result? [Likely]
  11. Did you keep a long-term holdout? What did it show compared with the initial test? [Likely]
  12. Tell me about an experiment result that surprised you. What did you do next? [Likely]
  13. Who made the launch decision, and what would have made you not launch? [Likely]

H. Impact and metrics

  1. What was the measured impact, in business terms? [Reported] [2][3]
    • How did you attribute the impact to your work and not to other launches?
  2. How did you ensure the users actually benefited after deployment? [Reported] [17]
  3. What metrics did you use to define success, and who agreed to them? [Reported] [2]
    • How did you align Product and Data Science on the success metric? [Likely]
  4. Were there negative side effects on another metric or another group (for example hosts versus guests)? [Likely]
  5. If the impact was smaller than expected, why? [Likely]

I. Challenges, failures and debugging

  1. What was the most challenging part of the project? [Reported] [16][15]
    • Was the challenge technical, organizational or about reliability?
  2. What technical challenges did you hit, and how did you solve them? [Reported] [6][14]
    • Where exactly is the technical challenge in this project? [Reported] [10]
  3. Tell me about a project or plan that missed its ETA, or whose scope had to shrink. [Reported] [9]
    • How did you identify the root cause? [Reported] [9]
    • Did you have a fallback plan to unblock the team? [Reported] [9]
  4. Tell me about a model that worked offline but failed in production. [Likely]
  5. Tell me about a bug or data problem that invalidated results. How was it found? [Likely]
  6. What was the hardest debugging problem on the project? [Likely]
  7. Tell me about a failure on this project and what you did next. [Reported] [3][4]

J. Planning, milestones, collaboration and conflict

  1. How did you plan the project? [Reported] [15]
    • How did you set and deliver milestones? [Reported] [17][11]
  2. How was the engineering effort structured? [Reported] [11][29]
  3. How did you persuade stakeholders to back the project? [Reported] [2]
    • Who opposed it, and what changed their mind?
  4. Did you have a conflict on the project? How did you resolve it? [Reported] [15][16][9]
    • What would the other person say about how it was resolved?
  5. How did you work with your teammates? [Reported] [16]
  6. Tell me about collaborating with another team where their delay affected you. [Reported] [9]
  7. How did you keep teams in different time zones or cultures aligned from design through launch? [Reported] [9]
  8. How did you handle a tight deadline or a complex project you owned? [Reported] [4]
  9. How did you work with data scientists on metric definitions and experiment reading? [Likely]
  10. How did you influence the technical direction without formal authority? [Likely]

K. Retrospective, learning and next steps

  1. If you redid the project, what would you change? [Reported] [15][17][3]
    • What would you change if you had to redesign it from scratch? [Reported] [17]
  2. What did you learn from the project? [Reported] [16]
  3. What would the next iteration of the system look like? [Likely]
  4. If you had half the time, what would you cut? [Likely]
  5. What technical debt did you leave behind, and why was it acceptable? [Likely]
  6. What was the biggest obstacle in moving from writing code to owning a whole project? [Reported] [9]

L. Leadership and senior/staff calibration

  1. Walk me through your experience, a past project and your leadership on it. [Reported] [21]
  2. How did you grow or mentor others on this project? [Likely]
  3. What is your current scope at work, and what scope are you looking for? [Reported] [10]
    • Are you looking to be a tech lead, or a strong IC? [Reported] [10]
  4. How did this project change how your team or organization works? [Likely]
  5. Which decision on this project would you defend most strongly to a skeptical staff engineer? [Likely]
  6. How did you decide what not to build? [Likely]

M. Mapping your project to Airbnb

  1. How does your system compare with how Airbnb solved a similar problem, and why did you choose your approach? [Reported] [24]
    • Have you read Airbnb's engineering blog posts on search ranking, pricing or experimentation? How does your work differ? [Likely] [24][30][31]
  2. How would your project transfer to a two-sided marketplace like Airbnb's (guests and hosts)? [Likely]
  3. What in your background maps directly onto this team's problems? [Reported] [20][2]
    • You have also worked in an unrelated area (for example hardware). How is that relevant here? [Reported] [20]
  4. If you joined, what would you build in your first six months, based on this experience? [Likely]
  5. Tell me about a project connected to your personal or professional values. How did it embody or challenge them? [Reported] [19][33]

N. Pressure probes reported in the room

These are the push-back styles candidates describe. Practise staying concise and concrete under each one.

  1. "I still don't understand. Can you explain the task again, more simply?" [Reported] [14]
  2. "Why did you do that at all?" (repeated challenges to the rationale for a phase of the project) [Reported] [10]
  3. "Can you be more specific? What were the actual technical details?" (asked because answers sounded generic) [Reported] [18]
  4. Interruptions and nitpicking mid-answer: how do you regain the thread? [Reported] [27]
  5. "Let's switch: how would you design an ML system for X?" (the experience round turns into ML design) [Reported] [7]
  6. "Give me the specific API and database design of your system." (detail-level probe rather than scaling talk) [Reported] [28]

08. Core Values Interviews: Champion the Mission, Be a Host, Embrace the Adventure, Be a Cereal Entrepreneur, plus Cross-Functional and Leadership Behavioral Questions

The chapter is therefore built from pages that could be read directly on GitHub. These include two TrueInterview-derived Airbnb process guides (one for the MLE track, one for the SWE track), a 2026 company guide that aggregates Hello Interview, interviewing.io, PracHub and LeetCode Discuss, the Tech Interview Handbook's Glassdoor-sourced Airbnb list, and a mirror of more than 100 1point3acres (1p3a) Airbnb interview posts. Every 1p3a post was downloaded and searched for values, behavioral (BQ), hiring-manager (HM) and culture content. Chinese-language posts are paraphrased in English here. Tags: [Reported] means a cited source says a candidate was asked this, or something very close to it, at Airbnb. [Likely] means an inference from the values, the Senior MLE / experimentation role, or Airbnb's products. It does not mean anyone reported it.

Format notes

Aspect What sources report
Number and length One core-values round of about 45 minutes is reported for the MLE track [1]. The SWE track reports one or two rounds of about 45 minutes each [2][3]. Hello Interview's G8 guide (as aggregated) calls it a "30-min mandatory gatekeeper" [4]. Rounds sometimes end early: one 2026 candidate finished in about 20 minutes and still passed [4][10]
Timing Often scheduled after the technical onsite has passed, as a separate gate [4][8][10]. One candidate had it after hiring-manager calls during team matching [18]
Interviewer An employee from any function, outside the hiring team, often a non-engineer [1][2][4][5][7]. One senior candidate's values round was with an engineering manager [15]
What is scored Authentic connection to Airbnb's four values: Champion the Mission, Be a Host, Embrace the Adventure, Be a "Cereal" Entrepreneur [5][12]. Personal stories count as much as work stories [2]. Answers that relabel engineering wins with value names, or that sound rehearsed or framework-driven, land poorly [1][2][3]. Rapport with the interviewer affects the outcome [1]
Weight Scored in the same debrief as the technical rounds [2]. It "can reject you on its own" [4][5]. One candidate failed two behavioral rounds and was told they "answered too quickly, without their own pace" [16]
Related rounds Experience / project deep dive (45-60 min): cross-functional influence, conflict, milestones and "who outside your team you had to align" are probed here. It drives leveling [1][2][17][18][19]. Hiring manager conversation: scope, past projects, team fit, and sometimes BQ questions about missed cross-team ETAs [1][8][20]
Senior bar No source defines a separate senior bar for the values round itself [1][2]. For Senior, scope, cross-team influence and ownership are judged mainly in the deep dive and HM rounds [1][2][20]. Expect values interviewers to probe for the same signals: mentoring, hosting newcomers, and leading through ambiguity [Likely]

Preparation signals from sources: prepare one or two stories per value, including non-work ones such as a cause you support, a time you hosted or built community, and feedback that stung [2][3][4]. Use plain language for a non-engineer interviewer [5]. Pace your answers and do not rush [16].

Questions

A. Opening, background and "Why Airbnb?"

  1. Walk me through your background. [Reported] [8]
    • What thread connects your career moves?
    • What do you do outside work?
  2. Why Airbnb? [Reported] [1][3][4][6][8][11]
    • Why now, and why this team rather than a bigger-name ML org?
    • What specifically drew you to Airbnb as a company, beyond the product? [Reported] [1]
  3. What do you think of Airbnb? [Reported] [6]
    • What does Airbnb do better than any other travel company?
  4. What excites you about the company? [Reported] [6]
  5. Tell me something about yourself and why you would be a good fit. [Reported] [6]
  6. What are your hobbies outside work, or what fun thing from school would you share? [Reported] [8]
  7. Is there anything you really want to do in the future? [Reported] [4]
    • Where do you want to be in five years, and how does Airbnb help?
  8. What is something you do not want to repeat from your last job? [Reported] [6]
  9. What obstacles, challenges or uncertainty do you expect if you join Airbnb? [Reported] [8]
    • What would make you leave Airbnb after a year?

B. Champion the Mission (belonging, community, causes)

  1. What does "belong anywhere" mean to you? [Reported] [6]
    • When have you felt you did not belong, and what changed it?
    • When have you created belonging for someone else?
  2. What part of our mission resonates most with you? [Reported] [6]
  3. How does Airbnb impact our guests and hosts? [Reported] [6]
    • How does it affect the neighborhoods and cities where hosts operate?
  4. What large problems in the world would you solve today? [Reported] [1][6]
    • Why that problem, and what have you personally done about it?
  5. What cause do you actively support, and how do you contribute to it? [Reported] [1][3]
    • How much time do you give it, and what have you learned from it?
  6. When did you last volunteer, and what did you take away from it? [Reported] [8]
  7. How have you contributed to a community, or what volunteer work have you done? [Reported] [4][5]
  8. Tell me a story about your community and the connections you have built. [Reported] [11]
  9. Describe a time you stood up for something you believe in. [Reported] [4]
    • What did it cost you, and would you do it again?
  10. Who has inspired you most, and why? [Reported] [11]
  11. What is Airbnb's hardest problem today, in your view? [Reported] [2]
    • How could ML or experimentation help solve it?
  12. Tell me about a project that reflected your personal or professional values. [Reported] [14]
    • How did the project embody or challenge those values?
  13. Name an organization (not Airbnb) that you admire, and explain why. [Reported] [4] (intern prompt, flagged unverified by the source)
  14. Tell me about a time you chose the right outcome for users even when an internal metric pointed the other way. [Likely]
    • In ML terms: when did you decline to ship a model that won the A/B test?
  15. How would you make sure a ranking or pricing model treats hosts and guests fairly across demographics and markets? [Likely]
    • How would you explain that trade-off to a non-technical interviewer?
  16. How do you keep the people affected by your models (hosts, guests, support agents) in mind when you only see aggregate metrics? [Likely]

C. Be a Host (care for guests, teammates and newcomers)

  1. Give me an example of when you have been a good host. [Reported] [2][6]
    • It does not have to be a listing: an event, a dinner, a visitor, a new hire? [Reported] [11]
  2. Do you have any experience as a host, of any kind? [Reported] [11]
  3. Tell me about your most memorable experience traveling with Airbnb. [Reported] [8]
    • What did the host do that made it memorable?
  4. What are your tips for choosing an Airbnb listing? Have you ever had a bad experience? [Reported] [8]
    • How would you fix that bad experience with product or ML?
  5. Share one of your trips with us. / Tell me about your most memorable trip. [Reported] [4][6]
  6. Name a situation where a company's customer service impressed you. [Reported] [6]
    • What would it take for Airbnb support to match it?
  7. What is the best gift you have ever given or received? [Reported] [6]
  8. If you had an unlimited budget to buy one gift for one person, what would you buy and for whom? [Reported] [6]
  9. Was there a senior colleague who helped you a lot early in your career? [Reported] [8]
    • How have you paid that forward?
  10. Tell me about a time you made a new teammate feel welcome and productive. [Likely]
    • What did you change in onboarding docs, tooling or pairing as a result?
  11. Describe a time you mentored an engineer or scientist to a measurable improvement. [Likely]
    • How did you adapt your approach to their style?
  12. Tell me about a time you went out of your way for an internal customer, such as a data scientist or product team that consumed your model or platform. [Likely]
  13. Tell me about a time you supported a teammate going through a hard period. [Likely]
  14. How do you make people who disagree with you feel heard in a design review or experiment readout? [Likely]
  15. Tell me about a time you had to give someone terrible news. [Reported] [5][6]
    • How did you prepare, and how did they react?

D. Embrace the Adventure (curiosity, risk, discomfort, resilience)

  1. What adventurous moments or decisions have you had in life? How do you take risks? [Reported] [8]
  2. Tell me about a calculated risk you took. [Reported] [2]
    • How did you size the downside before you committed?
  3. Tell me about a time you were uncomfortable and how you dealt with it. [Reported] [5][6]
  4. If you had an unlimited budget and could go anywhere, where would you go? [Reported] [6]
  5. What is something new you can teach your interviewer in a few minutes? [Reported] [6]
  6. Tell me about a self-learning experience: how you learned something, and whether it helped your later work. [Reported] [13]
  7. What is the most challenging project, in or out of work, that you have done recently? [Reported] [6][11]
  8. How do you manage the gap between expectations and reality, at work or in life? [Reported] [8]
  9. Tell me about a time a major change you did not choose (reorg, layoff, pivot) hit your team. How did you respond? [Likely]
  10. Tell me about a time you worked in a domain you knew nothing about, for example moving from recommendation to causal inference. [Likely]
    • How quickly were you useful, and what did you get wrong first?
  11. Tell me about an experiment or model idea that failed. What did you learn, and what did you do next? [Likely]
    • How did you tell the team?
  12. When have you changed your mind because of data that contradicted a strong belief? [Likely]
  13. If you were a gerbil, which gerbil would you be? (a reported curveball used to test spontaneity) [Reported] [6]

E. Be a "Cereal" Entrepreneur (resourcefulness, scrappiness, creativity)

  1. Tell me about a time you were scrappy and built something valuable with very limited resources. [Likely] [5][12]
    • What did you deliberately leave out?
  2. What is something you would like to remove from the Airbnb experience? [Reported] [5][6]
    • How would you test whether removing it helps?
  3. Tell me about a time you saw an unowned problem and took it on without being asked. [Likely]
  4. Describe a time you shipped a quick heuristic or a small experiment instead of waiting for the perfect model. [Likely]
    • How did you know when it was time to replace it?
  5. Tell me about a time you found a creative workaround for a data, compute or labeling constraint. [Likely]
  6. Have you ever started something of your own, such as a side project, club, startup or open-source tool? [Likely]
    • What did it teach you about customers?
  7. Tell me about a time you turned a one-off analysis or script into a tool other teams adopted. [Likely]
  8. Tell me about a time you reduced cost (infrastructure, experiment duration, labeling spend) without hurting quality. [Likely]
    • Which metrics showed it, and who signed off?
  9. Pitch me one product or ML idea for Airbnb that nobody has asked you to build. [Likely]
    • How would you validate it in two weeks?

F. Feedback, failure and self-reflection

  1. Have you given or received hard feedback? Tell me about it. [Reported] [2][3][11]
    • What did you change afterwards, and how would the other person describe it?
  2. Have you received negative feedback from others, and how did you handle it? [Reported] [13]
  3. Tell me about a time you did not meet expectations, and how you improved using customer feedback. [Reported] [13]
  4. What could you have done better in a past experience? [Reported] [8]
  5. What was your most difficult task, and how did you get through it? [Reported] [11]
  6. Tell me about a time you failed. [Likely] [3]
    • What would you do differently, and what did you change in your process?
  7. What would your last manager say is your biggest area for growth? [Likely]
  8. Tell me about a time you made a mistake that affected users or a launch, such as a bad model push or a wrong experiment readout. [Likely]
    • How did you communicate it, and what guardrail did you add?

G. Cross-functional collaboration and communication

  1. Give an example of communicating with your own team or with a different team. [Reported] [8]
  2. How do you make sure a design lands well with teams in different time zones or cultural backgrounds? What problems came up? [Reported] [8]
  3. In an urgent situation, how do you make sure you get time with senior people? [Reported] [8]
  4. How did you work with senior management on large projects and with multiple internal teams? [Reported] [6]
  5. Tell me about a time another team's internal ETA slipped and affected you, so the timeline moved or scope shrank. [Reported] [8]
    • How did you identify the root cause? [Reported] [8]
    • How was work split across the team? [Reported] [8]
    • Did you have a backup plan to unblock yourself? [Reported] [8]
  6. Show me a project you are proud of that crossed organizations or functions. [Reported] [17]
    • How were milestones set, how was engineering structured, and what conflicts arose? [Reported] [17]
  7. Tell me about a cross-team collaboration challenge on a past project. [Reported] [18]
  8. Tell me about a conflict, failure, tight deadline or complex project you owned. [Reported] [4]
  9. How did you set milestones on your project, and how did you ensure system quality and user benefit after deployment? [Reported] [19]
    • What would you change if you redesigned it? [Reported] [19]
  10. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product manager about the success metric for an experiment. [Likely]
    • Whose metric won, and why?
  11. Tell me about a time a data scientist and you read the same A/B test differently. How was it resolved? [Likely]
  12. How do you explain a model's behavior or an experiment's uncertainty to a non-technical stakeholder, such as an operations or policy partner? [Likely]
    • Explain a confidence interval to me as if I were your values interviewer.
  13. Tell me about a time you influenced a decision with no formal authority over the people making it. [Likely]
  14. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a partner team's request. [Likely]
  15. How do you work with designers and PMs when a model's output changes the user experience, for example in ranking or pricing suggestions? [Likely]
  16. Describe a time you resolved friction between two teammates or two teams. [Likely]

H. Senior-level ownership and leadership (MLE and experimentation flavor)

  1. What is the biggest obstacle in moving from writing code to owning an entire project? [Reported] [8]
  2. What is your current scope, and what scope do you expect here? [Reported] [20]
    • Do you want to be a tech lead, or a strong IC on a team that already has one?
  3. What was the hardest technical or organizational challenge you faced? [Reported] [20]
  4. Tell me about a time you set technical direction for a group larger than your own team. [Likely]
  5. Tell me about a time you stopped a launch, or argued against shipping, because the experiment evidence was weak. [Likely]
    • How did leadership react, and what happened next?
  6. Tell me about a time you raised the bar for experimentation rigor on your team, for example with pre-registration, guardrails or a review process. [Likely]
  7. How do you balance short-term booking or revenue metrics against long-term guest and host trust? [Likely]
  8. Tell me about a time you had to deliver an inconclusive or negative result to a team that badly wanted a win. [Likely]
  9. How do you grow other engineers' judgment about when ML is the wrong tool? [Likely]
  10. Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete data under time pressure. [Likely]
  11. How do you prioritize when three teams all need your help this quarter? [Likely]
  12. Tell me about a time you handed off something you built so that it no longer depended on you. [Likely]

I. Hiring-manager conversation (behavioral portion)

  1. Walk me through your most interesting project. What were the key design points and challenges? [Reported] [20][21]
  2. Which problems on our roadmap map onto your background, and where would you need to ramp up? [Reported] [1]
  3. Who outside your team did you have to align on your biggest project, and how? [Reported] [1][2]
  4. How did you scope work when requirements changed mid-project? [Reported] [2]
  5. Why did you choose a long-term plan over a short-term fix, and how did you justify it? [Reported] [20]
  6. Which metrics did you add yourself, and which existed already? [Reported] [20]
  7. What do you need from a manager to do your best work? [Likely]
  8. What kind of team culture do you create when you are the most senior IC in the room? [Likely]
