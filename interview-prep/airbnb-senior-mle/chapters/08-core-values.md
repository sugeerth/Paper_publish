# 08. Core Values Interviews: Champion the Mission, Be a Host, Embrace the Adventure, Be a Cereal Entrepreneur, plus Cross-Functional and Leadership Behavioral Questions

> Research note (read first). The shared web-search budget ran out before this chapter was written, so no new search-engine queries were possible. DNS lookups also failed for glassdoor.com, leetcode.com, interviewing.io, hellointerview.com, prepfully.com, igotanoffer.com, medium.com, careers.airbnb.com and news.airbnb.com, and reddit.com refused fetches. The chapter is therefore built from pages that could be read directly on GitHub. These include two TrueInterview-derived Airbnb process guides (one for the MLE track, one for the SWE track), a 2026 company guide that aggregates Hello Interview, interviewing.io, PracHub and LeetCode Discuss, the Tech Interview Handbook's Glassdoor-sourced Airbnb list, and a mirror of more than 100 1point3acres (1p3a) Airbnb interview posts. Every 1p3a post was downloaded and searched for values, behavioral (BQ), hiring-manager (HM) and culture content. Chinese-language posts are paraphrased in English here. Tags: **[Reported]** means a cited source says a candidate was asked this, or something very close to it, at Airbnb. **[Likely]** means an inference from the values, the Senior MLE / experimentation role, or Airbnb's products. It does not mean anyone reported it.

## Format notes

| Aspect | What sources report |
|---|---|
| Number and length | One core-values round of about 45 minutes is reported for the MLE track [1]. The SWE track reports one or two rounds of about 45 minutes each [2][3]. Hello Interview's G8 guide (as aggregated) calls it a "30-min mandatory gatekeeper" [4]. Rounds sometimes end early: one 2026 candidate finished in about 20 minutes and still passed [4][10] |
| Timing | Often scheduled **after** the technical onsite has passed, as a separate gate [4][8][10]. One candidate had it after hiring-manager calls during team matching [18] |
| Interviewer | An employee from **any function**, outside the hiring team, often a non-engineer [1][2][4][5][7]. One senior candidate's values round was with an engineering manager [15] |
| What is scored | Authentic connection to Airbnb's four values: Champion the Mission, Be a Host, Embrace the Adventure, Be a "Cereal" Entrepreneur [5][12]. Personal stories count as much as work stories [2]. Answers that relabel engineering wins with value names, or that sound rehearsed or framework-driven, land poorly [1][2][3]. Rapport with the interviewer affects the outcome [1] |
| Weight | Scored in the same debrief as the technical rounds [2]. It "can reject you on its own" [4][5]. One candidate failed two behavioral rounds and was told they "answered too quickly, without their own pace" [16] |
| Related rounds | **Experience / project deep dive** (45-60 min): cross-functional influence, conflict, milestones and "who outside your team you had to align" are probed here. It drives leveling [1][2][17][18][19]. **Hiring manager conversation**: scope, past projects, team fit, and sometimes BQ questions about missed cross-team ETAs [1][8][20] |
| Senior bar | No source defines a separate senior bar for the values round itself [1][2]. For Senior, scope, cross-team influence and ownership are judged mainly in the deep dive and HM rounds [1][2][20]. Expect values interviewers to probe for the same signals: mentoring, hosting newcomers, and leading through ambiguity [Likely] |

Preparation signals from sources: prepare one or two stories per value, including non-work ones such as a cause you support, a time you hosted or built community, and feedback that stung [2][3][4]. Use plain language for a non-engineer interviewer [5]. Pace your answers and do not rush [16].

## Questions

### A. Opening, background and "Why Airbnb?"

1. Walk me through your background. [Reported] [8]
   - What thread connects your career moves?
   - What do you do outside work?
2. Why Airbnb? [Reported] [1][3][4][6][8][11]
   - Why now, and why this team rather than a bigger-name ML org?
   - What specifically drew you to Airbnb as a company, beyond the product? [Reported] [1]
3. What do you think of Airbnb? [Reported] [6]
   - What does Airbnb do better than any other travel company?
4. What excites you about the company? [Reported] [6]
5. Tell me something about yourself and why you would be a good fit. [Reported] [6]
6. What are your hobbies outside work, or what fun thing from school would you share? [Reported] [8]
7. Is there anything you really want to do in the future? [Reported] [4]
   - Where do you want to be in five years, and how does Airbnb help?
8. What is something you do not want to repeat from your last job? [Reported] [6]
9. What obstacles, challenges or uncertainty do you expect if you join Airbnb? [Reported] [8]
   - What would make you leave Airbnb after a year?

### B. Champion the Mission (belonging, community, causes)

10. What does "belong anywhere" mean to you? [Reported] [6]
    - When have you felt you did not belong, and what changed it?
    - When have you created belonging for someone else?
11. What part of our mission resonates most with you? [Reported] [6]
12. How does Airbnb impact our guests and hosts? [Reported] [6]
    - How does it affect the neighborhoods and cities where hosts operate?
13. What large problems in the world would you solve today? [Reported] [1][6]
    - Why that problem, and what have you personally done about it?
14. What cause do you actively support, and how do you contribute to it? [Reported] [1][3]
    - How much time do you give it, and what have you learned from it?
15. When did you last volunteer, and what did you take away from it? [Reported] [8]
16. How have you contributed to a community, or what volunteer work have you done? [Reported] [4][5]
17. Tell me a story about your community and the connections you have built. [Reported] [11]
18. Describe a time you stood up for something you believe in. [Reported] [4]
    - What did it cost you, and would you do it again?
19. Who has inspired you most, and why? [Reported] [11]
20. What is Airbnb's hardest problem today, in your view? [Reported] [2]
    - How could ML or experimentation help solve it?
21. Tell me about a project that reflected your personal or professional values. [Reported] [14]
    - How did the project embody or challenge those values?
22. Name an organization (not Airbnb) that you admire, and explain why. [Reported] [4] (intern prompt, flagged unverified by the source)
23. Tell me about a time you chose the right outcome for users even when an internal metric pointed the other way. [Likely]
    - In ML terms: when did you decline to ship a model that won the A/B test?
24. How would you make sure a ranking or pricing model treats hosts and guests fairly across demographics and markets? [Likely]
    - How would you explain that trade-off to a non-technical interviewer?
25. How do you keep the people affected by your models (hosts, guests, support agents) in mind when you only see aggregate metrics? [Likely]

### C. Be a Host (care for guests, teammates and newcomers)

26. Give me an example of when you have been a good host. [Reported] [2][6]
    - It does not have to be a listing: an event, a dinner, a visitor, a new hire? [Reported] [11]
27. Do you have any experience as a host, of any kind? [Reported] [11]
28. Tell me about your most memorable experience traveling with Airbnb. [Reported] [8]
    - What did the host do that made it memorable?
29. What are your tips for choosing an Airbnb listing? Have you ever had a bad experience? [Reported] [8]
    - How would you fix that bad experience with product or ML?
30. Share one of your trips with us. / Tell me about your most memorable trip. [Reported] [4][6]
31. Name a situation where a company's customer service impressed you. [Reported] [6]
    - What would it take for Airbnb support to match it?
32. What is the best gift you have ever given or received? [Reported] [6]
33. If you had an unlimited budget to buy one gift for one person, what would you buy and for whom? [Reported] [6]
34. Was there a senior colleague who helped you a lot early in your career? [Reported] [8]
    - How have you paid that forward?
35. Tell me about a time you made a new teammate feel welcome and productive. [Likely]
    - What did you change in onboarding docs, tooling or pairing as a result?
36. Describe a time you mentored an engineer or scientist to a measurable improvement. [Likely]
    - How did you adapt your approach to their style?
37. Tell me about a time you went out of your way for an internal customer, such as a data scientist or product team that consumed your model or platform. [Likely]
38. Tell me about a time you supported a teammate going through a hard period. [Likely]
39. How do you make people who disagree with you feel heard in a design review or experiment readout? [Likely]
40. Tell me about a time you had to give someone terrible news. [Reported] [5][6]
    - How did you prepare, and how did they react?

### D. Embrace the Adventure (curiosity, risk, discomfort, resilience)

41. What adventurous moments or decisions have you had in life? How do you take risks? [Reported] [8]
42. Tell me about a calculated risk you took. [Reported] [2]
    - How did you size the downside before you committed?
43. Tell me about a time you were uncomfortable and how you dealt with it. [Reported] [5][6]
44. If you had an unlimited budget and could go anywhere, where would you go? [Reported] [6]
45. What is something new you can teach your interviewer in a few minutes? [Reported] [6]
46. Tell me about a self-learning experience: how you learned something, and whether it helped your later work. [Reported] [13]
47. What is the most challenging project, in or out of work, that you have done recently? [Reported] [6][11]
48. How do you manage the gap between expectations and reality, at work or in life? [Reported] [8]
49. Tell me about a time a major change you did not choose (reorg, layoff, pivot) hit your team. How did you respond? [Likely]
50. Tell me about a time you worked in a domain you knew nothing about, for example moving from recommendation to causal inference. [Likely]
    - How quickly were you useful, and what did you get wrong first?
51. Tell me about an experiment or model idea that failed. What did you learn, and what did you do next? [Likely]
    - How did you tell the team?
52. When have you changed your mind because of data that contradicted a strong belief? [Likely]
53. If you were a gerbil, which gerbil would you be? (a reported curveball used to test spontaneity) [Reported] [6]

### E. Be a "Cereal" Entrepreneur (resourcefulness, scrappiness, creativity)

54. Tell me about a time you were scrappy and built something valuable with very limited resources. [Likely] [5][12]
    - What did you deliberately leave out?
55. What is something you would like to remove from the Airbnb experience? [Reported] [5][6]
    - How would you test whether removing it helps?
56. Tell me about a time you saw an unowned problem and took it on without being asked. [Likely]
57. Describe a time you shipped a quick heuristic or a small experiment instead of waiting for the perfect model. [Likely]
    - How did you know when it was time to replace it?
58. Tell me about a time you found a creative workaround for a data, compute or labeling constraint. [Likely]
59. Have you ever started something of your own, such as a side project, club, startup or open-source tool? [Likely]
    - What did it teach you about customers?
60. Tell me about a time you turned a one-off analysis or script into a tool other teams adopted. [Likely]
61. Tell me about a time you reduced cost (infrastructure, experiment duration, labeling spend) without hurting quality. [Likely]
    - Which metrics showed it, and who signed off?
62. Pitch me one product or ML idea for Airbnb that nobody has asked you to build. [Likely]
    - How would you validate it in two weeks?

### F. Feedback, failure and self-reflection

63. Have you given or received hard feedback? Tell me about it. [Reported] [2][3][11]
    - What did you change afterwards, and how would the other person describe it?
64. Have you received negative feedback from others, and how did you handle it? [Reported] [13]
65. Tell me about a time you did not meet expectations, and how you improved using customer feedback. [Reported] [13]
66. What could you have done better in a past experience? [Reported] [8]
67. What was your most difficult task, and how did you get through it? [Reported] [11]
68. Tell me about a time you failed. [Likely] [3]
    - What would you do differently, and what did you change in your process?
69. What would your last manager say is your biggest area for growth? [Likely]
70. Tell me about a time you made a mistake that affected users or a launch, such as a bad model push or a wrong experiment readout. [Likely]
    - How did you communicate it, and what guardrail did you add?

### G. Cross-functional collaboration and communication

71. Give an example of communicating with your own team or with a different team. [Reported] [8]
72. How do you make sure a design lands well with teams in different time zones or cultural backgrounds? What problems came up? [Reported] [8]
73. In an urgent situation, how do you make sure you get time with senior people? [Reported] [8]
74. How did you work with senior management on large projects and with multiple internal teams? [Reported] [6]
75. Tell me about a time another team's internal ETA slipped and affected you, so the timeline moved or scope shrank. [Reported] [8]
    - How did you identify the root cause? [Reported] [8]
    - How was work split across the team? [Reported] [8]
    - Did you have a backup plan to unblock yourself? [Reported] [8]
76. Show me a project you are proud of that crossed organizations or functions. [Reported] [17]
    - How were milestones set, how was engineering structured, and what conflicts arose? [Reported] [17]
77. Tell me about a cross-team collaboration challenge on a past project. [Reported] [18]
78. Tell me about a conflict, failure, tight deadline or complex project you owned. [Reported] [4]
79. How did you set milestones on your project, and how did you ensure system quality and user benefit after deployment? [Reported] [19]
    - What would you change if you redesigned it? [Reported] [19]
80. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a product manager about the success metric for an experiment. [Likely]
    - Whose metric won, and why?
81. Tell me about a time a data scientist and you read the same A/B test differently. How was it resolved? [Likely]
82. How do you explain a model's behavior or an experiment's uncertainty to a non-technical stakeholder, such as an operations or policy partner? [Likely]
    - Explain a confidence interval to me as if I were your values interviewer.
83. Tell me about a time you influenced a decision with no formal authority over the people making it. [Likely]
84. Tell me about a time you had to say no to a partner team's request. [Likely]
85. How do you work with designers and PMs when a model's output changes the user experience, for example in ranking or pricing suggestions? [Likely]
86. Describe a time you resolved friction between two teammates or two teams. [Likely]

### H. Senior-level ownership and leadership (MLE and experimentation flavor)

87. What is the biggest obstacle in moving from writing code to owning an entire project? [Reported] [8]
88. What is your current scope, and what scope do you expect here? [Reported] [20]
    - Do you want to be a tech lead, or a strong IC on a team that already has one?
89. What was the hardest technical or organizational challenge you faced? [Reported] [20]
90. Tell me about a time you set technical direction for a group larger than your own team. [Likely]
91. Tell me about a time you stopped a launch, or argued against shipping, because the experiment evidence was weak. [Likely]
    - How did leadership react, and what happened next?
92. Tell me about a time you raised the bar for experimentation rigor on your team, for example with pre-registration, guardrails or a review process. [Likely]
93. How do you balance short-term booking or revenue metrics against long-term guest and host trust? [Likely]
94. Tell me about a time you had to deliver an inconclusive or negative result to a team that badly wanted a win. [Likely]
95. How do you grow other engineers' judgment about when ML is the wrong tool? [Likely]
96. Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete data under time pressure. [Likely]
97. How do you prioritize when three teams all need your help this quarter? [Likely]
98. Tell me about a time you handed off something you built so that it no longer depended on you. [Likely]

### I. Hiring-manager conversation (behavioral portion)

99. Walk me through your most interesting project. What were the key design points and challenges? [Reported] [20][21]
100. Which problems on our roadmap map onto your background, and where would you need to ramp up? [Reported] [1]
101. Who outside your team did you have to align on your biggest project, and how? [Reported] [1][2]
102. How did you scope work when requirements changed mid-project? [Reported] [2]
103. Why did you choose a long-term plan over a short-term fix, and how did you justify it? [Reported] [20]
104. Which metrics did you add yourself, and which existed already? [Reported] [20]
105. What do you need from a manager to do your best work? [Likely]
106. What kind of team culture do you create when you are the most senior IC in the room? [Likely]

## Deepening pass (October 2026): newly reported questions

Five web searches were run for this pass (English and Chinese phrasing; Blind, Reddit, LeetCode Discuss, 1point3acres, PracHub and prep-guide sites). No search snippet quoted a specific core-values question that a candidate said they were asked, so **no new [Reported] questions** and no retags of existing [Likely] items were possible. The searches did add these format signals:

- A 1point3acres thread is titled "Airbnb Senior MLE Core Value Interview Experience and Timeline". Its indexed summary says it covers focus areas, communication tips, how the round affects the result, and the post-interview timeline. The question text was not visible [22]. This confirms that a dedicated core-values round exists for the Senior MLE track.
- A Blind thread on the core values round says it is not about technical expertise and that examples can come from work or personal life. Repliers suggested reading the values and watching the CEO's interviews, with an emphasis on how you help others [23].
- A Blind SWE write-up reports passing the Core Values round and then entering team match. This fits the "values gate before team match" order in the format table [24].
- Prep guides (not candidate reports) say the values interviewer usually has not seen your resume, and that one or two values rounds may be run by trained employees outside the hiring team [25][26].

### Additional [Likely] questions closing gaps for Senior MLE

107. What was the most difficult decision you have made, at work or in life, and how did you make it? [Likely] [25]
     - What did you give up, and would you decide the same way today?
108. Tell me about a time something did not go as planned and you had to rebuild the plan with the team. [Likely] [25]
     - How did you keep morale and trust while you replanned?
109. Tell me about a time you protected guests or hosts from harm, such as fraud, safety issues or a privacy risk, even though it slowed a launch. [Likely]
     - How did you weigh false positives that hurt legitimate hosts?
110. When have you pushed back on using personal or sensitive data in a model, and how did you resolve it? [Likely]
111. Airbnb is adding AI and LLM features to support and trip planning. How would you make sure an AI feature feels like being hosted rather than being handled? [Likely]
     - Tell me about a time automation made a user experience worse, and what you did.
112. Tell me about a time you helped build a more diverse or inclusive team, for example through interviewing, hiring or mentoring. [Likely]
     - How do you check your own bias as an interviewer?
113. How do you build connection and belonging on a team that is mostly remote or spread across time zones? [Likely]
114. Tell me about a time you tried to persuade people and failed. What did you learn about influence? [Likely]
115. Tell me about a time you put your team's or company's goals ahead of your own project or credit. [Likely]
     - How did you feel about it, and how did others notice?
116. Which of Airbnb's four values is hardest for you personally, and when did you fall short of it? [Likely]
